# Start in Fano, finish at Bologna's bonfire

**Fly Emirates into Bologna on Saturday 26 December and take the train straight to Fano. Spend three nights there with your friend, using the town as a base for Urbino and the Frasassi caves. On Tuesday 29 December, ride back to Bologna together for four nights: a day trip to Venice on Wednesday 30, New Year's Eve on Thursday 31 at the city's free bonfire of the "Vecchione" in Piazza Maggiore, and the flight home from the same airport on Saturday 2 January.** I score that route **8.5/10**. It has the shortest transfers (about two hours each way between Fano and Bologna), the most distinctive and least stressful New Year's Eve of any candidate, and Italy's best food city. It also gives you Venice in daylight without Venice's New Year prices, on a simple return ticket. Three alternatives score **8** and each wins under one condition. End in **Venice** if the lagoon fireworks matter enough to pay €500–1,000+ for that night and you can fly home from Venice. End in **Rome** if you can fly home from Rome and want the mildest weather (12–13°C), the easiest halal food and Friday prayers at the Grande Moschea on 1 January. End in **Florence** if Renaissance art comes first. Milan (6), Verona (7) and a Dolomites snow detour (6) trail for this particular week. Expect cold, short, often grey days: highs of about 8–9°C in Bologna, 10–11°C in Fano and 6–7°C in Venice, with frost possible across the north (the same week last year brought −3 to −5°C nights to the Po Valley) and only about nine hours of daylight. This winter's strong El Niño leans mild and wet, but no forecast for your dates means anything until about **18–20 December**. Two jobs come first. Confirm that Emirates flies to Bologna on Saturdays this winter: it runs five flights a week, not daily as earlier notes said, and both your travel days are Saturdays. Then book flexible fares and free-cancellation rooms, because westbound flights cross Saudi airspace and no policy bought now will cover the Gulf escalation, which is already public. With economy flights and a shared room, the week should cost about **USD 2,700–4,500 per person**, which fits your budget even at peak-week prices.

## Three nights in Fano and four in Bologna make the strongest week

A word on evidence before the plan. The research team could not open most web pages this session, so nearly every finding below comes from search-engine summaries of the cited pages, not full reads. Most 2026–27 holiday programmes (New Year's Eve line-ups, Christmas events, museum holiday hours) had not been published by 9 November. Last year's (2025–26) editions stand in for them and are labelled "last year". Hotel rates, airfares and several journey times are estimates and are flagged as such. The firmest numbers in this report are the weather normals and last year's observed temperatures, which came straight from AccuWeather's climatology tool. The scores are my judgement against your brief. As the earlier reports noted, Christmas to New Year is the most expensive week of the Italian winter. Your dates are fixed by the friend, so this plan works within them. Fano's low-season rooms are the main offset.

The route works because the trip is anchored by an airport, a friend and a holiday calendar, and Bologna fits all three. Direct trains run from Bologna to Fano in about **1h49–2h07** ([Trainline](https://www.thetrainline.com/train-times/bologna-to-fano)), with about 40 trains a day ([Omio](https://www.omio.it/treni/bologna/fano)). Both transfers are therefore short, and your friend's ride home on 1 January is easy. Bologna's New Year's Eve is free, central and unlike any other. Last year it was a party in Piazza Maggiore from 23:00, then at midnight the burning of an artist-designed "Vecchione" ("old man") standing for the departing year, then a DJ. The tradition in its modern form dates from 1922 ([Bologna Welcome](https://www.bolognawelcome.com/en/blog/cosa-fare-a-capodanno-a-bologna); [VoloScontato](https://www.voloscontato.it/sagre-ed-eventi/tutto-sul-capodanno-2026-a-bologna-info-dove-e-programma.php)). There are no Venice-style checkpoints and no tickets, the hotel is a short walk away, and the night centres on the bonfire, not alcohol. Venice is **1h15 away by non-stop Frecciarossa** ([Trainline](https://www.thetrainline.com/train-times/bologna-to-venice)). You get Italy's most beautiful city on a weekday when the Doge's Palace is open, without paying Venice's New Year's Eve rates: an estimated **€450–1,000+** for a 4-star room that night, against **€250–400** in Bologna. And you fly home from the airport you landed at.

Four awkward days shape the plan. **Saturday 26 December is Santo Stefano**, a public holiday. Most sources say museums and sites open while shops close ([Italy Perfect](https://www.italyperfect.com/plan-your-trip/practical-information/holidays-closing-days-italy.php)), which makes it a good day for the flight and the train to Fano. **Monday 28 December** is when many Italian state museums close or cut their hours. Urbino's Palazzo Ducale was listed at 08:30–14:00 on Mondays in an older listing ([MuseiOnline](https://www.museionline.info/marche-musei-monumenti/galleria-nazionale-delle-marche)), so Urbino goes on Sunday and Monday becomes the caves day. **Thursday 31 December** is a normal sightseeing day until early evening; the Ferrari Museum in Maranello opens every day except Christmas Day and New Year's Day ([Ferrari Museum brochure](https://cdn.ferrari.com/cms/network/media/pdf/museum-brochure-2024-eng-v2.pdf)). Long-distance trains thin out that evening, but this plan has no travel on 31 December. **Friday 1 January** is the heaviest closure day of the year, so it is for a lie-in, a walk and, if you pray, Jumu'ah. One guide names a mosque on Via Curtatone as the anchor of Bologna's Muslim community ([HalalSpy](https://halalspy.com/muslim-travel/europe/italy/)), but confirm the address and time locally. Bologna's festive look lasts all week. Last year the Via d'Azeglio art lights ran from 8 December into March ([Il Resto del Carlino](https://www.ilrestodelcarlino.it/bologna/cronaca/luminarie-via-dazeglio-pedonale-imagine-accensione-quando-l9i9juuh)), and the Via Altabella Christmas fair ran until 6 January ([VoloScontato](https://www.voloscontato.it/sagre-ed-eventi/mercatini-di-natale-di-bologna-2025-quando-dove-e-info.php)). One gap to accept: the **Asinelli tower is closed**, with 2028 the "optimistic" reopening ([Il Resto del Carlino](https://www.ilrestodelcarlino.it/bologna/cronaca/bologna-welcome-in-rosso-buco-c6811727)).

Two swaps keep the shape intact. If your friend has a car, Monday can be the long Frasassi-plus-Gubbio day. Without one, do Frasassi alone by train and keep Gubbio for another trip. If you would rather slow down, drop the Venice day and add a fourth night in Fano, with San Marino and Gradara by car on 29 December, then move to Bologna on 30 December. You lose the most beautiful city of the trip, but you gain a festive hill-town evening. If Venice looks foul on 30 December, swap it for Modena, Ferrara or Ravenna's indoor mosaics. Don't move Venice to the 31st: the trip back on New Year's Eve is tiring.

## Venice, Rome and Florence each score 8, and each wins on one condition

Each alternative below keeps the same first half (three Fano nights) and changes only the finale. You and your friend can therefore decide the second half last, once the friend's preference, Emirates' winter schedule and the December programmes are known.

**Venice** is the most beautiful choice and the most expensive one. Last year's New Year's Eve fireworks over St Mark's Basin were free, with security checks and controlled access at the viewing points. The city's own guidance was that they **cannot be seen from Piazza San Marco**. The best view is near the Arsenale vaporetto stop on the Riva degli Schiavoni ([Venezia Unica](https://www.veneziaunica.it/en/node/2396)), so expect hours standing in the cold at a checkpointed waterfront. La Fenice's ticketed New Year concert cost €25–500 last year ([Teatro La Fenice](https://www.teatrolafenice.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/1-com-Capodanno-2026_.pdf)); one unverified page puts this year's on 31 December at 16:00. The Doge's Palace closes on 25 December and 1 January ([Vacatis](https://vacatis.com/it/doges-palace-opening-hours)), and no access fee applies (the reported 2027 calendar starts 26 March, per [Pixidia](https://pixidia.com/?p=92545)). The sketch is: travel on 29 December, sightsee on the 30th and 31st, fireworks at midnight, a walking and lagoon day on 1 January, then fly out of Venice on the 2nd. Fano to Venice by train takes about **3–3.5 hours**. That is my sum of the two sourced legs; an earlier estimate of 2h10–2h40 looks too optimistic. Last year Trenitalia ran 16 extra regional trains out of Venice after midnight ([Trenitalia](https://www.trenitalia.com/it/chi-siamo/comunicati-stampa/treni-straordinari-per-il-capodanno-2026-a-venezia.html)), but none to Bologna, so seeing in the New Year in Venice means sleeping there.

**Rome** is the strongest finale for a halal-observant traveller and the hardest one to reach. It is the mildest city on the list, with the best halal food, centred on Esquilino and Termini ([HalalSpy Rome](https://halalspy.com/halal-food-guide/europe/rome/)). Because 1 January is a Friday, you can attend Jumu'ah at the Grande Moschea; tourist visits are not possible on Fridays ([Centro Astalli](https://www.centroastalli.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Grande-Moschea-di-Roma.pdf)). Last year's papal calendar had Vespers and the **Te Deum at 17:00 on 31 December** and Mass at 10:00 on 1 January in St Peter's ([Holy See Press Office](https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/bollettino/pubblico/2025/12/06/251206e.html)). Tickets are free and requested from the Prefecture of the Papal Household ([Vatican News](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/pope/news/2025-09/pope-leo-s-liturgical-celebrations-for-november-through-january.html)). The costs are real, though. Fano to Rome takes **3h57 at best and 5h10 on average** ([Rome2rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Fano/Roma-Termini)), or 4.5–5.5 hours door to door, which leaves about 2.5 days in Rome. Last year the Vatican Museums closed on 26 December and 1 January ([Turismo Roma](https://www.turismoroma.it/en/events/orari-musei-e-mostre-natale-2025-capodanno-e-epifania-2026)). The Colosseum's 1 January opening is uncertain: the culture ministry listed it as closed, but the park's own site says it opened on 1 January 2026 ([MiC](https://cultura.gov.it/evento/natale-al-parco-archeologico-del-colosseo); [colosseo.it](https://colosseo.it/?p=53385)). The free Circo Massimo concert, headlined last year by Alessandra Amoroso, Fabri Fibra and Tananai ([Turismo Roma](https://www.turismoroma.it/en/news/new-years-concert-2026-circus-maximus)), was **not yet in the 2026–27 calendar as of 24 October** ([Pixidia](https://pixidia.com/?p=92645)). Rome also has a city fireworks ban ([GreenMe](https://www.greenme.it/?p=1247343)). There is a less obvious reason to end in Rome. If the Gulf closes again, the 2 January return is the hardest seat to rebook, and Rome has three daily Emirates flights plus Etihad and ITA, against five weekly flights from Bologna (an inference from the schedules below).

**Florence** is the art finale and is close to your airport: 37 minutes from Bologna by Frecciarossa ([Trenes.com](https://www.trenes.com/it/treno-bologna-firenze/)) and about **2h35** from Fano by train ([Omio](https://www.omio.it/viaggi/fano/firenze-7c4hb)). Last year's New Year's Eve was a free "festa diffusa" across four piazzas: a main concert by Tiromancino with Paola Iezzi, gospel at SS. Annunziata, jazz at the Carmine, and light shows with the countdown in the Signoria ([SiViaggia](https://siviaggia.it/notizie/capodanno-2026-firenze/570433/)). Sky TG24 placed the main stage in the Signoria instead ([Sky TG24](https://tg24.sky.it/lifestyle/2025/12/23/capodanno-2026-firenze-cosa-fare)); check the ordinance in December. The Uffizi closes on Mondays, 25 December and 1 January ([La Nazione](https://www.lanazione.it/cosa-fare/musei-aperti-natale-capodanno-toscana-bb73fac1)). Arriving on the 29th sidesteps the Monday, and the Uffizi and Accademia then fit on 30–31 December. The big Santa Croce Christmas market ends around 20–21 December ([VoloScontato](https://www.voloscontato.it/sagre-ed-eventi/mercatini-di-natale-a-piazza-santa-croce-a-firenze-nel-2025.php)), and halal food is limited to a few spots near the Mercato Centrale, Santa Maria Novella station and Sant'Ambrogio ([HalalSpy Florence](https://halalspy.com/halal-food-guide/europe/florence/)).

## Fano is a base, and its best days are 40–90 minutes away

Fano rewards one unhurried day, not three. The **Arch of Augustus** (9 AD) and the Augustan walls that still enclose part of the centre ([Wikivoyage](https://it.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Fano)), the 15th-century **Rocca Malatestiana** and the Museo Civico's Roman archaeology ([IlTurista](https://www.ilturista.info/guide/fano-marche-mare-spiaggia-cosa-vedere/)) fill a morning. Opening hours for the museums and the Rocca's interior were not found, so ask the tourist office. This year's Christmas programme is confirmed as **"Il Natale Più – Natale nella Terra di Mare", 28 November 2026 to 6 January 2027** ([Il Resto del Carlino Fano](https://www.ilrestodelcarlino.it/fano/cronaca/ora-dobbiamo-portare-il-mondo-5f424c80)). Last year it included a Christmas market at the Pincio, open 09:00–20:00, and a Santa village until 6 January ([Cose di Casa](https://www.cosedicasa.com/mercatini/mercatino-di-natale-di-fano)). Last year's New Year's Eve was a free double music event in **Piazza XX Settembre** with Radio Studio Più ([Il Resto del Carlino Fano](https://www.ilrestodelcarlino.it/fano/cronaca/un-capodanno-per-tutti-la-a450356e)). The town's signature dish is **brodetto alla fanese**, a "white" Adriatic fish stew cooked in vinegar and oil, at about €25–40 for a meal. It traditionally ends with a **moretta**, espresso with rum, brandy and anise ([Storie Gastronomiche](https://storienogastronomiche.it/?p=95460)). Gambero Rosso praises the brodetto at **Alla Lanterna** in Metaurilia ([Gambero Rosso](https://www.gamberorosso.it/notizie/rubriche/itinerari/alla-scoperta-delle-marche-piu-vere-dal-brodetto-di-fano-allolio-di-cartoceto)). **AlMare**'s prize-winning version must be ordered ahead ([Gambero Rosso](https://www.gamberorosso.it/?p=117981)), so book both before you fly.

If your friend's circle has its own New Year plans, Fano's piazza party is the local option. Three AccuWeather values should not be trusted. **Florence's normals of 14–15 / 7°C** sit well above other sources, which put Florence's December at about 12°C by day and 4°C at night ([Weather2Travel](https://www.weather2travel.com/december/italy)), so plan for 11–12 / 2–4°C. **Ortisei's observed lows of −14 to −23°C** are implausible for a village at about 1,200 m; the data point probably sits on a high-altitude grid cell. **Fano's normal low of 5–6°C** is higher than Pesaro climate data, which give average January lows of about 2°C ([climieviaggi](https://www.climieviaggi.it/clima/italia/pesaro)). In plain terms: on the Fano coast expect about 9–12°C by day and 2–6°C at night, with rain on roughly one day in three. In Bologna, Verona, Venice and Milan expect about 5–9°C by day and around freezing at night. Rome is mildest at 12–13°C, and the Dolomites range from about −10 to +3°C.

Last year shows why the normals understate the risk. The forecast for that New Year's Eve was still "uncertain" between high pressure and "arctic cold with snow at relatively low altitudes" on 25 December 2025 ([Centro Meteo Italiano](https://www.centrometeoitaliano.it/notizie-meteo/meteo-capodanno-rimane-incerto-tra-il-freddo-artico-con-neve-a-quote-relativamente-basse-e-una-perturbazione-polare-25-12-2025-175990/)). The cold arrived: from 30 December to 3 January the Po Valley cities saw **lows of −3 to −5°C and highs of only 2–4°C**, Fano dropped to −4°C on 1 January, and Cortina stayed at −4°C or colder all day from New Year's Eve onwards. This winter's seasonal signal points the other way. An early-November NOAA update, as summarised by MeteoGiornale, has a **strong to very strong El Niño lasting at least through January–March 2027** ([MeteoGiornale](https://www.meteogiornale.it/2026/11/meteo-news/el-nino-sempre-piu-forte-litalia-deve-preoccuparsi/)). Italian press coverage of the ECMWF and Copernicus seasonal runs shows Europe "almost entirely" above average for December–February, with **below-average snowfall in most lowland areas in December** ([Sky TG24](https://tg24.sky.it/cronaca/2026/10/15/previsioni-inverno-2026-2027-neve)). One source puts the winter about 1°C milder than the 1996–2015 average ([Napolike](https://www.napolike.it/come-sara-inverno-2026-2027-previsioni-italia)). A composite of 70 years of El Niño winters suggests rainy spells into December and **possible cold outbreaks from January**, and it explicitly says it is "not a forecast" ([MeteoGiornale](https://www.meteogiornale.it/2026/09/meteo-news/el-nino-2026-27-gli-effetti-su-italia-ed-europa-spiegati-bene/)). The TV meteorologist Giuliacci instead expects a dry November–December and a much colder January–February ([Napolike](https://www.napolike.it/super-el-nino-mai-visto-che-inverno-sara-in-italia-le-previsioni-di-giuliacci)). The fair summary: lean mild and wet, add about 1°C to the normals, and keep a 2025-style arctic week in mind, especially for 1–2 January.

**Day-specific forecasts become reliable only about 7–10 days out.** For you, that means checking ilMeteo, 3BMeteo, MeteoAM or AccuWeather from about **18–20 December** to decide the Venice day and any snow outing. New Year's Eve itself firms up only around 26–28 December, three to five days ahead. Venice's tide forecast is good only two to three days ahead. Three local hazards are worth knowing. **Po Valley fog** is far rarer than it was: Bologna's winter fog hours near the airport fell 47% between 1984–94 and 2004–13 ([Il Post](https://www.ilpost.it/2022/12/08/meno-nebbia-di-una-volta-pianura-padana/)). It still mainly threatens early-morning flights and motorway driving, not high-speed trains. **Acqua alta**: the MOSE barriers now rise for forecasts of 110 cm ([Il Post](https://www.ilpost.it/2024/09/16/alzare-mose-venezia/)), but St Mark's Square floods from about 80 cm ([Europe for Visitors](https://www.europeforvisitors.com/venice/articles/acqua-alta.htm)), so the realistic Venice risk is a few hours of wet feet. The **bora** and north-easterly gales occasionally hit the Fano–Pesaro coast with sea surges; in October 2025 Fano activated its emergency operations centre ([MeteoWeb](https://www.meteoweb.eu/2025/10/maltempo-marche-attivati-i-centri-operativi-comunali-a-san-benedetto-del-tronto-e-fano/1001845609/)).

**Packing:** bring layers that work down to about −5°C, a waterproof shell and waterproof shoes with grip for wet stone and Urbino's slopes. Pack or buy overboots if you are in Venice on a high-tide day. You will stand outdoors for an hour or more at midnight, so bring a warm hat, gloves and a scarf. Plan hill towns for the morning and lights for the evening: the sun sets at about 16:30–16:50 everywhere on this list.

## Flights, trains and the Gulf are where this plan can break

### Emirates flies Bologna five times a week, so check Saturdays first

**Emirates is the only Gulf airline with a nonstop to Bologna**, flown by an A350 with Premium Economy ([FlightConnections](https://www.flightconnections.com/flights-from-dxb-to-blq); [The Flight Club](https://www.theflightclub.it/en/2026/01/emirates-a350-rome/)). The earlier notes, citing reporting from January 2026, called the route daily. The newer reporting says frequency **rose from four to five a week on 1 June 2026** ([The Flight Club](https://www.theflightclub.it/en/2026/06/emirates-venice-bologna/amp/); [Quality Travel](https://www.qualitytravel.it/emirates-aumenta-le-frequenze-a-venezia-e-bologna/120981)). The newer figure wins, and no winter 2026–27 schedule was found. Two weekdays a week may therefore have no flight. Your outbound (26 December) and return (2 January) are both Saturdays, so one check on emirates.com settles both. If Saturday is not served, you have three fixes: shift both legs by a day; fly into Venice instead (about 3–3.5 hours to Fano); or fly an open-jaw, into Bologna and out of Venice, Rome or Milan, which Emirates sells on one ticket. No Gulf airline flies to Ancona or Rimini, the airports nearest Fano ([Skyscanner](https://www.skyscanner.ch/ch/it-it/chf/rotte/aoi/dxba/ancona-per-dubai.html)). Emirates also flies to Rome three times daily on the A380, to Milan Malpensa three times daily, and to Venice on a 777-300ER with First Class. Etihad flies Abu Dhabi to Rome in about 6h15 ([Etihad](https://www.etihad.com/flights-from-abu-dhabi-to-rome)), and flydubai flies twice daily to Milan Bergamo ([WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c1grq1m-flydubai-strengthens-operations-italy-with)).

No live fares for these dates were found. The researchers estimate **AED 3,500–5,500 (USD 950–1,500) return in economy** to Bologna and AED 13,000–22,000 (USD 3,500–6,000) in business. They base this on Kayak's figures, which show December as the dearest month for Dubai–Italy at an average of AED 3,052 ([Kayak AE](https://www.kayak.ae/flight-routes/Dubai-Intl-DXB/Italy-IT0)), and on late-December UAE fares running 30–50% above off-peak ([Travel And Tour World](https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/9fr4i1lzi5wk/)). The 2 January return is the expensive leg. It lands in the home-bound rush at the end of the UAE school break, which runs 14 December–3 January with schools back on 4 January ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/education/uae-three-year-school-academic-calendar-explained)).

### Westbound flights cross Saudi airspace, and insurance will not help

The risk to this trip is leaving and re-entering the UAE, not Italy. UAE airports are operating, and westbound flights to Europe "typically head south, away from Iran, as soon as they take off, then head west over Saudi Arabia" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/travel/2026/03/24/why-flights-from-the-uae-are-taking-longer-and-costing-more)). That route carries its own exposure. A Houthi-run body declared Saudi airspace "not safe" on 5 October ([GNA](https://gna.org.gh/2026/10/yemens-houthis-declare-saudi-airspace-unsafe-for-airlines/)), and Riyadh airport was struck and only partly reopened on 25 October ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/travel/2026/10/25/saudi-arabia-flight-cancellations-which-airlines-are-suspending-services-and-until-when/)). The broader picture has worsened since late October. US officials reportedly told Gulf allies to prepare for strikes on Iran after the midterms ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/usiranisrael-war-what-uae-residents-need-to-know-today-oct-29-2026-1.500727683)), and on 7 November the US embassy for Iran warned of cancellations and airspace closures ([US Virtual Embassy Iran](https://ir.usembassy.gov/security-alert-iran-november-7-2026/)). EASA's Gulf conflict-zone bulletin runs to **16 November**, with no extension seen yet ([EASA](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/air-operations/czibs/czib-2026-07r3)). The US rates the UAE Level 3, "Reconsider travel" ([US State Department](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/united-arab-emirates.html)). The realistic worst case is February's: **UAE airspace closed for about 48 hours**, and Qatar's and Bahrain's for about 144 ([FlySafe](https://flysafe.zone/cases/gulf-airspace-closures-february-2026/)). That would mean lost days and extra hotel nights, not danger in Italy.

Insurance cannot carry this risk. Once a conflict is publicised, insurers treat it as foreseeable, so a policy bought now will not cover this escalation ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/travel/middle-east-airspace-crisis-puts-travel-insurance-war-exclusions-to-the-test-567274.aspx)), and standard policies exclude war-caused cancellations anyway ([Wego](https://blog.wego.com/does-travel-insurance-cover-flight-cancellations-caused-by-war/)). Your protection is the ticket itself. Buy a **flexible fare directly from Emirates** with every leg on one ticket. Keep hotels on free cancellation. Commit nothing non-refundable until EASA has decided on 16 November. Returning on 2 January leaves only one buffer day before the school week, so build in more slack if work allows. Entry is simple. A UAE passport has long given visa-free Schengen access for 90 days in any 180 (not re-verified in this research). The EES biometric system has been live since 10 April 2026, so expect fingerprints and a photo at your first entry ([France Diplomatie](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/en/presse-et-ressources/decouvrir-et-informer/actualites/ees-le-nouveau-systeme-europeen-de-gestion-des-frontieres-entre-en-service-le-10-avril-2026)), and queues at holiday arrivals. ETIAS is not operating and will not apply in December ([PAX News](https://www.paxnews.com/news/airline/etias-no-longer-has-confirmed-launch-date)).

### Trains are easy, strikes are banned, and New Year's Eve is a no-travel night

The notes give different Bologna–Fano times, and here is the explanation. Direct Intercity and regional trains take about **1h49–2h07** ([Trainline](https://www.thetrainline.com/train-times/bologna-to-fano)); Virail's fastest is 1h47 ([Virail](https://www.virail.it/treni-bologna-fano)) and Omio's average is 2h01 ([Omio](https://www.omio.it/treni/bologna/fano)). A direct Frecciarossa reaches **Pesaro in about 1h23** ([Trainline](https://www.thetrainline.com/train-times/bologna-to-pesaro)), and Fano is 7 minutes further ([Omio](https://www.omio.it/treni/stazione-di-pesaro/stazione-di-fano-gzy7a)). The faster figure of "1h22–1h26 with one change" from about €38.65 ([Trainline](https://www.thetrainline.com/train-times/bologna-centrale-to-fano)) cannot be right if the Pesaro leg alone takes 1h23. Treat it as an aggregator artefact, or as a Frecciarossa stop at Fano that nobody verified. Plan on **about two hours of train time** and 3–3.5 hours from landing to Fano. Every travel day falls inside the Christmas–Epiphany strike-free period under Law 146/1990. Pixidia gives it as 18 December–7 January ([Pixidia](https://pixidia.com/?p=76226)), while the earlier notes had 17 December–7 January; the official text was not read. Either way, national transport strikes should not be called that week. Re-check the strike register in mid-December anyway. Trains run on holiday timetables on 26 December and 1 January, and the Adriatic line is thinner than the Bologna–Venice and Bologna–Florence trunk. The researchers expect the last long-distance trains on 31 December around 21:00–22:00 and gaps on the morning of 1 January, from typical patterns rather than this year's timetables. Both the recommended route and the alternatives therefore reach the New Year's Eve city by the 29th and avoid early trains on the 1st. Frecciarossa and Italo tickets open about four months ahead, and cheap fares for 27–30 December and 2 January sell first. Buy the changeable fares, given the airspace uncertainty.

Some things stay genuinely unknown until December, and the plan above is built to absorb them. Nobody yet knows which artists play which piazzas, whether Rome's Circo Massimo concert happens, whether Milan's reported concert is real, or when this year's Vatican and Uffizi holiday hours appear. Nor has anyone published a firm Bologna winter flight schedule, a live fare or a hotel quote for 31 December. All hotel and fare figures in this report are estimates. Fano's museum hours, the Pesaro and Ancona mosque listings and the Frasassi 1 January tour slots also remain unconfirmed. None of these gaps changes the recommended route, which depends on none of them. They mainly affect how good the alternatives look in mid-December.

## Conclusion

The question looked like "which fourth city?", but the airport you fly home from decides most of it. Flying home from Bologna points to Bologna or Florence, flying from Venice points to Venice, and flying from Rome points to Rome. The one fact that settles this, Emirates' Saturday schedule at Bologna, is a two-minute check this week. The research also reverses a common assumption. The city you land in turns out to be the best place for New Year's Eve, and Venice works better as a Wednesday day trip than as a midnight crush. Fano itself is less a destination than a launch pad, and the friend's car matters more to the week than any museum booking.

This week's risks also don't line up with its pleasures. Italy's own disruptions are unusually muted, with strikes legally barred and lowland snow forecast below average. The fragile parts are the 2 January return seat and an Iran escalation that no insurer will now cover. That argues for spending budget on flexibility rather than luxury. It also gives one asymmetric reason to consider ending in Rome or Milan: more daily flights home if you need to rebook. Decide the second half with your friend in mid-December, once the programmes and forecasts are out, and book the first half now.
