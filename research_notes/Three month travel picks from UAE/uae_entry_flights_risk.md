## 1. Entry rules for UAE passport holders, destination by destination (incl. health requirements)

### Takeaway
For an **Emirati ordinary passport**, **visa-free or visa-on-arrival** covers:
- **Indian Ocean:** Maldives, Mauritius, Seychelles (the Seychelles also needs an online Travel Authorisation).
- **Southeast Asia:** Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Philippines, Indonesia (visa on arrival).
- **Southern Africa:** South Africa, Botswana, Namibia (visa on arrival).
- **Middle East and North Africa:** Morocco, Egypt, Jordan.
- **Europe:** Spain/Schengen.

**Sri Lanka's ETA has been free for UAE nationals since 25 May 2026**, but it must still be obtained before travel.

**Online visas or authorisations are needed** for:
- Vietnam (e-visa, ~3 working days).
- India, for a first-time visitor (e-visa). Visa on arrival is only for Emiratis who have held an Indian visa before.
- Kenya (eTA, USD 30).
- Tanzania and Uganda (e-visas, ~USD 50).
- Bhutan (visa USD 40 plus SDF USD 100/night, valid to 31 Aug 2027).

**Nepal, Rwanda, Cambodia and Egypt have conflicting or unclear sources.** Verify them before booking.

**Yellow fever:** a direct flight from the UAE (a non-endemic country) generally does **not** trigger a certificate requirement for Tanzania or Rwanda. A **connection through an endemic country** such as Kenya, Ethiopia or Uganda can trigger one.

**Health / yellow fever**
- **Tanzania:** a certificate is needed only when arriving from, or transiting for more than 12 h through, a yellow-fever-risk country. **The UAE is not a risk country, so a direct flight is usually exempt.** Kenya counts as endemic for Tanzania's rule, so a Kenya stopover or a combined Kenya–Tanzania safari can trigger it. Older reports said "all entrants" needed a certificate, so the current rule should be checked — [SafariFind](https://safarifind.com/blog/tanzania-vaccinations-health-rules-for-emiratis-2026); [Fragomen (older "all entrants" alert)](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/alerts/yellow-fever-certificate-now-required-all-entrants); [Nature Discovery PDF](https://www.naturediscovery.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Yellow_Fever_entry_requirements_in_Tanzania_mainland.pdf)
- **Rwanda [OFFICIAL, undated]:** no certificate for travellers from non-endemic countries without an active outbreak. Travellers from endemic or outbreak countries need one, or can be vaccinated on entry — [Rwanda Biomedical Centre](https://www.rbc.gov.rw/index.php?id=745)
- **Kenya / Uganda:** no source found for UAE arrivals. East African states jointly require proof of immunisation from risk-country arrivals — [PrecisionVaccinations](https://www.precisionvaccinations.com/yellow-fever-vaccination-required-enter-kenya-tanzania-uganda-rwanda-burundi-or-sudan); [The EastAfrican](https://theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/sustainability/east-africa-tightens-travel-to-contain-yellow-fever-spread-1419820)

### Inferences
- **Lowest-friction options for a one-week trip.** No pre-arrival paperwork beyond an arrival card:
  - Maldives, Mauritius, South Africa, Botswana, Morocco, Egypt, Jordan, Spain.
  - Malaysia, Singapore, Philippines and Thailand (each needs a digital arrival card).
  - Seychelles (online Travel Authorisation) and Sri Lanka (free ETA) are similar, after a quick online step.
- **Destinations needing paperwork days or weeks ahead:**
  - Vietnam (~3 working days).
  - Kenya (eTA).
  - Tanzania and Uganda (e-visas; allow 1–2 weeks [BK]).
  - India, for a first visit (e-visa).
  - Bhutan (visa plus SDF, booked with a licensed operator or the official portal).
- **Bhutan cost check:** a 6-night trip costs USD 600 in SDF plus a USD 40 visa per person before hotels, guide and flights. That fits a USD 4,000+ budget, but leaves less headroom than elsewhere.
- **Universal rules [BK]:** have at least 6 months' passport validity and blank pages (South Africa wants 2 blank pages [BK]), plus proof of onward travel.
- **Malaria [BK]:** prophylaxis advice applies to East Africa safari areas, northern Namibia, Botswana (Okavango/Chobe), and parts of Cambodia, Indonesia and the Philippines. It is a health precaution, not an entry rule.

## 2. Nonstop routes and block times from DXB / AUH / SHJ; which destinations need connections

### Takeaway
**Nonstop from the UAE:**
- **South Asia and Indian Ocean:** Sri Lanka, Maldives, India, Nepal, Mauritius, Seychelles.
- **Southeast Asia:** Thailand (Bangkok, Phuket, Krabi), Vietnam (Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City), Cambodia (Phnom Penh, Etihad), Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia (Jakarta, Bali, Medan), Philippines.
- **East Africa:** Kenya, Tanzania (Dar es Salaam, Zanzibar, Kilimanjaro), Uganda.
- **Southern Africa:** South Africa (Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban).
- **North Africa, Middle East and Europe:** Morocco (Casablanca), Egypt, Jordan, Spain (Madrid, Barcelona).

**One connection needed:** **Bhutan** (via Kathmandu, Delhi, Bangkok or Kolkata), **Namibia** and **Botswana** (via Johannesburg or Doha), **the Canary Islands and most of Andalusia** (via Madrid, Barcelona or Istanbul), and probably **Rwanda**.

**Block times are mostly 3–9 h.** They should be largely unaffected by Iran-avoidance, which mainly lengthens **northbound** flights to Europe, the Caucasus and Central Asia. **Westbound** flights to Egypt, Jordan, Morocco and Spain cross Saudi airspace, which the Houthis declared "not safe" on 5 Oct 2026.

## 6. Long-haul sanity check: Australia, New Zealand, Patagonia, Brazil (Rio Carnival 2027), Mexico/Caribbean

## 7. Travel insurance: changes to war or airspace-closure exclusions for UAE departures in late 2026

### Takeaway
**No insurer change dated October or November 2026 was found.** The prevailing position, as of a September 2026 guide, is:
- Standard policies **exclude war-caused cancellations**, and a ceasefire or its collapse does not change that.
- Airspace closures depend on the cause and the wording.
- **Cancel-for-any-reason (CFAR) cover** typically reimburses **50–75%** of non-refundable costs.
- **Foreseeability is the trap.** Once a conflict or advisory is widely publicised, it is generally treated as a known event, so new policies bought now **will not cover disruption from the current US–Iran escalation**.

### Cited Findings
- Standard travel insurance usually does **not** cover war-related flight cancellations, and "a ceasefire — or its breakdown — does not change the war exclusion". Whether an airspace closure is excluded "depends on [its] cause and the policy wording". CFAR reimburses **50–75%**. Travellers are advised to get the insurer's answer in writing. This is a September 2026 guide that cites the EASA bulletin CZIB-2026-07R2 — [Wego](https://blog.wego.com/does-travel-insurance-cover-flight-cancellations-caused-by-war/)
- Travel delay or interruption sections may respond to government- or airline-driven closures, provided the underlying cause is not an excluded war risk.
