## 1. Season fit by month and region

### Takeaway
In this window, the **mainland "cool–dry" belt is at its best**: Bangkok, Chiang Mai, the Andaman coast (Phuket/Krabi), Cambodia/Angkor, Laos and south Vietnam. So is the **Philippines from roughly mid-December** (dry *amihan* season). The **wet side of the region** is the Gulf of Thailand (Koh Samui) until about mid-December, central Vietnam (Hoi An/Hue) through November and much of December, Malaysia's east-coast islands (closed), Borneo, Bali/Java, and Komodo from about January. North Vietnam is dry but **cool, grey and sometimes cold** in December–January.

### Inferences
**Climatology by country and month [BK, standard climate norms, unsourced in this session]**

- **Thailand:**
  - **Bangkok and the north (Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai, Pai):** the cool–dry season runs Nov–Feb.
    - Bangkok: December highs about 31–32 °C, lows about 21–23 °C.
    - Chiang Mai in December–January: highs about 28–29 °C, nights about 13–16 °C, with chilly mountain mornings (Doi Inthanon can approach single digits).
    - Chiang Mai's **burning-season haze** usually builds from **February** and peaks in **March–April**. Early February is usually acceptable but is the start of the risk period.
  - **Andaman coast (Phuket, Krabi/Railay, Koh Lanta, Khao Lak, Phi Phi):**
    - Dry season with calm seas roughly **Nov/Dec–Apr**. Late November can still bring showers. Seas are best from December.
    - The Similan Islands park is normally open about **15 Oct–15 May**.
  - **Gulf of Thailand (Koh Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao):**
    - Wettest **Oct–Dec**, with **November usually the peak rain month**.
    - It improves from about late December and is good **Jan–Apr**.
    - So Samui is a poor pick for late November to early December and a strong one in January to early February.
- **Vietnam has three climates:**
  - **North (Hanoi, Ha Long/Lan Ha Bay, Ninh Binh, Sapa, Ha Giang):**
    - November is one of Hanoi's best months: dry, about 20–27 °C.
    - **December–January** is cool to cold and often overcast with drizzle: Hanoi highs about 18–20 °C, lows about 13–15 °C.
    - **Sapa and Ha Giang can drop to about 0–5 °C** with occasional frost.
    - **Ha Long Bay** cruises run, but mist cuts visibility.
  - **Central (Hue, Da Nang, Hoi An; also Quy Nhon and Nha Trang):** wet and flood-prone through November and often much of December. It improves in **January** and is good from **February**; February to April is best.
  - **South (Ho Chi Minh City, Mekong Delta, Phu Quoc, Con Dao):** the dry season runs **Dec–Apr**. **Phu Quoc is at its best Nov/Dec–Mar.**
- **Cambodia:**
  - The dry season runs Nov–Apr. **Nov–Jan is the "cool" peak** at Angkor: highs about 30–32 °C, early mornings about 20–22 °C.
  - It is hotter from February (about 33–34 °C).
  - The south-coast islands (Koh Rong, Koh Rong Samloem) are best **Dec–Apr**.
- **Laos:**
  - **Luang Prabang and Vientiane** are dry Nov–Feb. Luang Prabang mornings are misty and cool (about 12–16 °C), with highs about 27–29 °C. **Dec–Jan is peak season.**
  - Agricultural-burning haze in northern Laos usually builds from about **February–March**.
- **Malaysia:**
  - **West coast:**
    - **Langkawi** is best **Dec–Mar** (dry, calm Andaman-side seas).
    - **Penang** is usually drier from about December.
    - **Kuala Lumpur** has afternoon storms all year, with an inter-monsoon peak around Oct–Nov.
  - **East coast** islands are closed (see Cited Findings).
  - **Borneo:**
    - Sabah (Kota Kinabalu, Kinabalu Park, Sepilok, the Kinabatangan river, Danum Valley) and Sarawak (Kuching, Bako, Mulu) are in the **northeast-monsoon wet season Nov–Feb/Mar**. Kuching is usually wettest around **January**, and the Kinabatangan can flood around December–January.
    - Wildlife viewing and Mt Kinabalu climbs still operate, but conditions are wetter.
    - Borneo's best months are roughly **Mar–Oct**.
- **Indonesia:**
  - **Bali** is in the wet season (**Nov–Mar**), with **Dec–Feb wettest**: heavy, often afternoon or evening downpours, high humidity, occasional street flooding in Kuta/Seminyak, and monsoon-borne rubbish on west-coast beaches. Ubud's culture and landscapes remain enjoyable. Bali's dry season (May–Sep) is clearly better.
  - **Java** (Yogyakarta with Borobudur and Prambanan, Mt Bromo, Ijen) is also wet. Bromo sunrise views are often clouded, and Ijen can close for weather.
  - **Komodo and Labuan Bajo:**
    - The best months are about **Apr–Nov**. The wet season starts around **December**, and **January–February bring rougher seas**.
    - Many liveaboards pause or reduce trips in January–February. Day trips still run.
- **Philippines:**
  - The dry *amihan* (northeast-monsoon) season runs **Dec–May**, and **Dec–Feb is the cooler, comfortable part**.
  - The **typhoon "tail"** covers November and early-to-mid December. Historic examples: Haiyan, 8 Nov 2013; Rai/Odette, 16 Dec 2021, which hit **Siargao and Bohol**.
  - **Palawan (El Nido, Coron)** is best **Dec–Apr**. Amihan winds can make El Nido's Bacuit Bay choppy and cancel some island-hopping tours in December–February.
  - **Boracay** is at its peak with calm White Beach in Dec–Feb.
  - **Siargao's** Pacific-facing east coast gets **northeast-monsoon rain Nov–Jan**; its best dry season is about Mar–Jun.
  - **Bohol and Cebu** are good Jan–May.
- **Singapore:**
  - The **northeast monsoon runs Dec–early Mar**, with December–January the wettest (short, intense showers), at 25–31 °C.
  - **February is noticeably drier.** Weather rarely ruins a city trip.

**What this means by month**
- **Late November** favours Bangkok + Chiang Mai, the Andaman coast (early season), Angkor, Laos, north Vietnam (best month) and south Vietnam. Avoid **central Vietnam, Koh Samui, Malaysia's east coast and Bali/Java at their wettest**. The Philippines carries a real typhoon risk.
- **December:** the whole mainland dry belt is excellent. The Philippines and Langkawi become good, especially after about 10–15 December. Hoi An is still risky early in the month. Bali and Borneo are wet.
- **January** is the **best all-round month**. Even central Vietnam is drying out, the Philippines is in prime season and Koh Samui is good. Bali, Komodo and Borneo are at their weakest.
- **Early February** brings rising heat on the mainland, the start of Chiang Mai/Laos haze, and good weather across the Philippines, central Vietnam, Samui and Singapore. **Lunar New Year (6 Feb) disrupts Vietnam and adds crowds and prices across the region** (Section 2).

## 2. Events in the window (Loy Krathong/Yi Peng, NYE, Lunar New Year/Tết, Sinulog, Thaipusam)

### Takeaway
**Loy Krathong 2026 falls on Tue 24 Nov, after mid-November and inside the window.** Yi Peng in Chiang Mai is about **23/24–25 Nov**. Other dates:
- **Sinulog** (Cebu): **Sun 17 Jan 2027**
- **Thaipusam**: **Fri 22 Jan 2027**, the big day at Batu Caves (KL), with Penang and Singapore as well
- **Lunar New Year/Tết**: **Sat 6 Feb 2027**. Vietnam's official Tết break is reportedly **Thu 4 Feb–Wed 10 Feb 2027**, and China's holiday is about **5–11 Feb**.

So the "early February" sub-window collides with Lunar New Year in Vietnam, Singapore and Malaysia.

### Inferences
**Loy Krathong / Yi Peng**
- **Loy Krathong on 24 Nov is the single best reason to go to Thailand in late November.**
- **Itinerary:** Chiang Mai for Yi Peng on about 23–25 Nov, then Bangkok's riverside Loy Krathong (Chao Phraya, ICONSIAM, Asiatique). Sukhothai Historical Park also hosts a famed light-and-krathong festival [BK].
- **Practicalities [BK/EST]:**
  - Mass lantern releases in Chiang Mai are now **ticketed private events** (e.g. at Mae Jo), with tickets from roughly USD 100 to several hundred.
  - Unregulated lantern releases are restricted around the city.
  - Chiang Mai airport usually **reschedules or cancels flights during the main lantern nights**.
  - Hotels in Chiang Mai's old city and Nimman sell out and reprice for those nights.
- Etihad's nonstop **Abu Dhabi–Chiang Mai** (Section 3) makes this unusually easy from the UAE.

**Same full moon elsewhere [BK, dates unconfirmed]**
- Laos's **That Luang Festival** (Vientiane) and Cambodia's **Water Festival (Bon Om Touk)** follow the same lunar cycle and *may* fall in the second half of November 2026.
- Hoi An's monthly **full-moon lantern night** falls around 23–24 Nov, 23–24 Dec and 21–22 Jan.

**New Year's Eve [BK]**
- Big set pieces:
  - **Bangkok**: ICONSIAM river fireworks and CentralWorld countdown
  - **Singapore**: Marina Bay countdown
  - **Kuala Lumpur**: KLCC/Merdeka 118 fireworks
  - **Bali**: beach clubs
  - **Manila**
- **Christmas**: the Philippines has the region's biggest, longest Christmas season (Simbang Gabi dawn masses 16–24 Dec).
- **NYE week is the most expensive of the window** everywhere (Section 3).

**Lunar New Year (6 Feb 2027) by country**
- **Vietnam:**
  - The **days before Tết (about 28 Jan–5 Feb)** see an exodus home, with trains, buses and domestic flights sold out and fares and taxis at a premium.
  - During **4–10 Feb**, many family restaurants, shops, markets and some museums close, especially for the first 3 days (6–8 Feb).
  - Hotels and tourist sites in Hoi An, Da Nang, Phu Quoc and Ha Long mostly stay open, but are busy and pricey with domestic travellers.
  - The flower markets and decorations of the pre-Tết week are charming. *Inference [BK]: a Vietnam trip should end by about 3 Feb, or avoid 4–10 Feb entirely.*
- **Singapore [BK/EST]:**
  - Chinatown street light-up and bazaar from mid-January, and **River Hongbao** around Marina Bay.
  - Public holidays are Sat 6 and Sun 7 Feb, *probably* with **Mon 8 Feb in lieu** (estimated).
  - Many hawker stalls and family shops **close 2–5 days** around 6–8 Feb. Malls and attractions stay open.
- **Malaysia [BK]:**
  - CNY public holidays on 6–7 Feb, with the biggest festivities in **Penang** (George Town, Kek Lok Si temple illuminations) and KL.
  - *Balik kampung* traffic jams, and some Chinese-run eateries close for several days.
- **Thailand:**
  - Not a national holiday, but there are big celebrations in Bangkok's Yaowarat and Phuket Town.
- **Region-wide (inference):**
  - Mainland Chinese, Korean and other North Asian holiday demand on **5–11 Feb** lifts prices and crowds in Bangkok, Phuket, Bali, Singapore, Da Nang/Nha Trang and Phu Quoc.

**Other January events in the Philippines and Malaysia**
- **Sinulog (17 Jan)** is the Philippines' biggest festival. In the same window are **Ati-Atihan** in Kalibo, Aklan (gateway to Boracay; also third Sunday of January, so 17 Jan 2027) and **Dinagyang** in Iloilo (fourth Sunday, 24 Jan 2027) [BK]. Cebu hotels sell out and reprice for the Sinulog weekend (inference).
- **Thaipusam (22 Jan)** is spectacular at Batu Caves: pre-dawn processions and *kavadi* bearers. It is extremely crowded and best viewed early. Penang's Waterfall Hilltop Temple and Singapore's Little India procession also mark it [BK].

**Gulf-side timing**
- The **UAE winter break (14 Dec–3 Jan)** and **Eid Al Etihad (2–3 Dec)** concentrate UAE outbound demand.
- The cheapest UAE departure window is about **10–31 Jan**, per the sibling notes. That overlaps Sinulog, Thaipusam and the best weather, which favours **mid-to-late January** as the sweet spot.

## 3. Costs: hotels in December–January peak, flight times and routes from the UAE, and what more than USD 4,000 per person buys

### Takeaway
Southeast Asia is the **best value in the whole "winter trip" field** for a budget above USD 4,000 per person including flights.
- **Economy flights:** **nonstop 6–9 h economy returns** leave most of the budget for 5-star or luxury hotels in every country, even at Christmas.
- **Business class** is realistic for most destinations outside the 20 Dec–3 Jan peak. This is an estimate.
- **Peak multipliers:** Phuket luxury rates run **2–3x normal** at Christmas–New Year, and Bali Christmas-week rates run **50–100% above standard, with 5–7-night minimums**.
- **Routes:** Etihad now flies **nonstop Abu Dhabi to Krabi, Chiang Mai and Phnom Penh**, which opens efficient one-week itineraries. **Singapore Airlines remains suspended on Dubai until 28 Feb 2027.**

**What the border situation means for a tourist (inference)**
- **Thailand–Cambodia:**
  - Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Phuket, Krabi, Samui, Siem Reap/Angkor, Phnom Penh and the Cambodian coast are **far outside the 20 km zones**. Siem Reap town is roughly 100+ km from the nearest disputed sections [BK].
  - **Do not plan** visits to border temples: Preah Vihear, the Ta Muen/Ta Moan group, Ta Krabei [BK].

  - The **renewed clashes of July and December 2025 show escalation can recur quickly**. Buy flexible tickets and insurance for any Thailand+Cambodia combination.
  - **Verdict:** a manageable risk for tourism, not a reason to skip either country.
- **Myanmar spillover [BK, unsourced this session]:**
  - The FCDO and others have long advised caution or against travel near parts of the **Thai–Myanmar border** (Tak/Mae Sot, Mae Hong Son border areas), linked to Myanmar's civil war and the scam-compound crackdown. Fighting near the border, stray shells and refugee flows have occurred.
  - This does not affect standard Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai or Golden Triangle day-trip itineraries, but **avoid border crossings into Myanmar** (e.g. Mae Sai–Tachileik).
- **Thailand's deep south [BK]:** the FCDO's long-standing advice also covers **Pattani, Yala and Narathiwat**. It is irrelevant to the standard tourist circuits, but note it if a Hat Yai or overland Malaysia link is considered.
- **Philippines:** Siargao, Palawan, Bohol, Cebu and Boracay are all outside the Level 3–4 areas. **Mainland Mindanao west and central (Marawi, Cotabato, Sulu, Basilan, Tawi-Tawi) is the no-go zone.**
- **Haze:**
  - **Expect mid-January PM2.5 spikes in Bangkok and Hanoi**, a pattern repeated most winters [BK]. A sensitive traveller should check AQI and prefer beaches or the islands in January.
  - **Chiang Mai, northern Laos and northern Thailand** become hazy from about **February–March**, so early February is borderline for Chiang Mai and Luang Prabang.
- **Arrival formalities [BK; another researcher covers these in depth]:**
  - **Thailand:** the **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)** has been **mandatory online within 3 days before arrival since 1 May 2025**.
  - **Malaysia:** the **MDAC** is required.
  - **Singapore:** the **SG Arrival Card** is required.
  - **Indonesia:** an electronic arrival card is required, plus e-VOA.
  - **Philippines:** **eTravel** is required.
  - **Malaysia** also charges a **tourism tax of RM10 per room per night** for foreign guests.
- **Natural hazards still live in the window:**
  - Late-season Philippine typhoons (November to mid-December)
  - Central Vietnam floods (November–December)
  - Lewotobi and other Indonesian volcano ash disruptions (any month)
  - Monsoon flooding in east-coast Malaysia, Kelantan/Terengganu and Borneo (December–January)
  - Indonesia earthquakes, such as the August 2026 event near Flores (cited above)
- **Crime and scams [BK]:** the ordinary risks are petty theft and scams (Bangkok gem/tuk-tuk scams, Bali motorbike accidents, drink spiking in party areas). The 2024 **Vang Vieng (Laos) methanol poisoning** that killed six foreign tourists [OLDER] argues for avoiding cheap spirits in backpacker hubs.

**Frank country verdicts (inference)**
1. **Thailand: go (8.5).**
   - **Pros:** one country gives temples, street food and Bangkok's luxury scene, the Lanna north and Andaman limestone seascapes. All of it is reachable nonstop from AUH or DXB (Bangkok, Phuket, Krabi and Chiang Mai via Etihad, Emirates and others). The weather is ideal Nov–Jan, and halal is easy in Bangkok, Krabi and Phuket.
   - **Cons:** Christmas–NYE price spikes; mid-January smog days in Bangkok; avoid the Gulf islands until about mid-December; no overland to Angkor. The 300-baht fee, if enacted, is trivial.
   - **Best one week:** **20–28 Nov** as Chiang Mai (Yi Peng) → Bangkok → Krabi, or **mid-to-late January** as Bangkok → Chiang Mai → Phuket/Krabi or Koh Samui.
2. **Philippines: go in January to early February (7.5).**
   - **Pros:** world-class island scenery (El Nido/Coron lagoons, Bohol's Chocolate Hills and tarsiers, Boracay) at its driest; Sinulog adds culture.
   - **Cons:** domestic connections eat time; weaker food and city appeal; limited halal; late-November and early-December typhoon risk; Christmas flights from the UAE are very expensive.
3. **Vietnam: go in mid-to-late January and exit before Tết (7).**
   - The food is the region's best, alongside Thailand's.
   - **Pros:** Hanoi, Ha Long/Lan Ha and Hoi An make a top culture-plus-nature week. Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City have nonstop flights (Etihad AUH–Hanoi was announced in Nov 2024, and its 2026 operation is unverified; Emirates DXB–Hanoi/HCMC [BK]).
   - **Cons:** north cold and grey in December–January; central Vietnam floods in November–December (record 2025 floods); **Tết 4–10 Feb 2027** wrecks early February; Hanoi smog; weak halal.
4. **Cambodia: go in December to January (7).**
   - **Pros:** Angkor at peak season is a bucket-list culture pick. Etihad's nonstop to Phnom Penh helps, and luxury is excellent value (Shinta Mani, Raffles, Amansara).
   - **Cons:** thinner for "nature/adventure" (Koh Rong and Kulen are modest); the border conflict means no Thailand overland combination and requires monitoring, though tourist areas are unaffected.
5. **Malaysia: good in January, especially for halal-observant travellers (7).**
   - **Pros:** the easiest Muslim-friendly trip (GMTI #1); KL + Penang food + Langkawi beaches; the best luxury value; Thaipusam spectacle on 22 Jan.
   - **Cons:** "nature" is weaker in this window because the east-coast islands are closed and Borneo is in its wet season.
6. **Singapore: superb add-on, so-so standalone (6.5; 8 as part of a combo).**
   - **Pros:** food, safety and halal (#1 non-OIC) are top tier.
   - **Cons:** expensive; little nature for a full week; **Singapore Airlines Dubai flights suspended to 28 Feb 2027** (Emirates only on DXB–SIN).
   - **Best use:** pair with Malaysia, Bintan or Bali.
7. **Indonesia/Bali: wrong season (6).**
   - **Pros:** Bali's culture (Ubud) and luxury resorts still work in the rain. Indonesia is GMTI joint #2, but Bali itself is Hindu-majority.
   - **Cons:** Dec–Feb is peak wet season, with Christmas surcharges and minimum stays; Komodo seas worsen from January; Lewotobi ash has disrupted flights in past years.
   - **Best:** late November if at all; otherwise April–October.
8. **Laos: lovely but small (6).**
   - **Pros:** Luang Prabang in December–January is magical (alms giving, Kuang Si falls, Mekong).
   - **Cons:** needs connections from the UAE; very limited halal; 2–3 nights is enough.
   - **Best use:** as an add-on to northern Thailand or Hanoi.
9. **Malaysian Borneo: wait for March–October (5.5).** Orangutans and Kinabalu are possible in the window, but it is wet season with river-flooding risk.

**Trip-wide watch-items for the writer**
1. **Gulf airspace/airport disruption risk at the UAE end** (US Level 3 for the UAE; FCDO escalation warning). Buy flexible fares and insurance.
2. **UAE school break 14 Dec–3 Jan**, the expensive window.
3. **Lunar New Year 5–11 Feb**, the crowded and expensive window.
4. **10–31 Jan** is the sweet spot on weather, price and events across most of the region.
