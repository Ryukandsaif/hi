## 1. Rome, 26 Dec – 2 Jan: what's open or closed, papal events, Christmas sights, New Year's Eve, crowds, halal

### Takeaway
Rome works well for this week. Most sites are open 27–31 Dec and 2 Jan. The Vatican Museums close on 26 Dec and 1 Jan (2025–26 pattern; 2026–27 not confirmed). The Colosseum closes on 25 Dec, and its 1 Jan opening is uncertain (it opened on 1 Jan 2026). The Christmas set pieces should still be running: the Piazza Navona market, the St Peter's Square crib and tree, and the Piazza del Popolo tree. New Year's Eve is good but not certain. In 2025–26 the city put on a free Circo Massimo concert, but the 2026–27 edition had not been announced by late October. The papal Te Deum (31 Dec) and the 1 Jan Mass are free with tickets. Halal food is easy to find in Rome. The drawback is the 4–5 hour trip each way from Fano.

## 2. Florence (+ Siena, Chianti, Pisa), 26 Dec – 2 Jan: museums, New Year's Eve in the piazzas, markets, day trips, halal

### Takeaway
Florence is the easiest add-on: 37 minutes from Bologna. Its New Year's Eve is a strong, free, multi-piazza "Capodanno diffuso", with a main stage plus gospel, jazz and light shows; the 2025–26 headliner was Tiromancino. Glass and fireworks are banned in the centre, which makes the night calmer.

There are two big caveats:
- **The main Santa Croce Christmas market closes around 20–21 Dec**, so it is gone before this week. Only smaller markets (Santa Maria Novella's ChristmARS) run to 6 Jan.
- **The Uffizi is closed on 25 Dec and 1 Jan, and on Mondays (Mon 28 Dec 2026)** unless a special holiday opening is announced.

Siena and Pisa also hold free New Year's Eve concerts (Irama and Alfa in 2025–26). Siena's Christmas market, however, is held in early December.

### Inferences
- **Museums:** plan the Uffizi for **Sat 26 Dec (if the special opening repeats), Sun 27, Tue 29, Wed 30 or Thu 31 Dec, or Sat 2 Jan**. Avoid Mon 28 Dec and Fri 1 Jan unless special openings are announced. The Accademia is likewise closed on Mondays, and 1 Jan is uncertain. Book timed slots now; the holiday week is the busiest of the winter.
- **Christmas markets:** Florence after Christmas means lights, the Duomo tree and nativity, and smaller markets. The big Santa Croce market has gone. For festive markets, Florence is weaker than Rome (Navona runs to 6 Jan) or the Alpine markets (Bolzano, Trento, to 6 Jan; see the earlier files).
- **New Year's Eve:** Florence is the most compact and walkable of the three, with several free stages within 10 minutes' walk of each other. The glass and fireworks bans make it safer and family-friendlier than Naples-style chaos (inference). Expect the 2026–27 format to be similar: the call for organisers goes out in autumn, and line-ups and ordinances are published in December.
- **Siena:** a good day trip on 27, 29 or 30 Dec, or 2 Jan. The Campo is atmospheric, but the Christmas market will be over. Siena's own Piazza del Campo concert (Irama in 2025–26) is an alternative New Year's Eve setting, but getting back to Florence after midnight is the problem. Pisa is the same: Alfa in 2025–26.
- **Chianti:** wine estates are the main draw, so the appeal depends on the traveller. Many small estates likely run reduced holiday hours (unverified).
- **Halal:** limited but workable near SMN, the Mercato Centrale and Sant'Ambrogio. Rome is far easier.

## 3. Getting there: Fano–Rome, Fano–Florence, Bologna–Florence–Rome; car vs train; ZTL; strike risk

### Takeaway
Fano sits on the Adriatic line, with no direct fast route to Rome:
- **Fano–Rome:** about 4–5 hours by train, either via Ancona/Falconara–Orte or via Bologna (the fastest about 3h35–4h, with backtracking north).
- **Fano–Florence:** about 2.5 hours by train via Bologna, or about 2–2.5 hours by car.
- **Bologna–Florence:** 37 minutes.
- **Florence–Rome:** roughly 1h20–1h35 (not verified this session).

Do not drive into either historic centre. ZTL cameras send fines of about €80–335 per entry, months later, plus rental-company fees.

## 4. Snow option: the Dolomites (Cortina, Val Gardena, Alta Badia, Madonna di Campiglio) vs Apennine resorts near Bologna and Fano

### Takeaway
New Year is the Dolomites' **peak high season**: lift passes are €89 a day, hotels are at their highest rates, and 3–7-night minimum stays are common. Snow is reasonably assured at altitude thanks to extensive snowmaking, and the 2026/27 season opens 28 Nov and 4 Dec. From Bologna, a detour takes about 4–5 hours each way (more from Fano), so it only makes sense with at least 3 nights there.

The Apennine resorts are about 1–1.5 hours from Bologna (Corno alle Scale, Cimone, Abetone; unverified estimate). They are small, low and snow-dependent: opening estimates range from 5 Dec to 25 Dec 2026, and openings have been delayed in some past seasons. They work as a weather-permitting snow-play day trip, not as a destination.

## 6. Overall: is Rome worth the extra travel from Fano on a 1-week trip vs a northern city? Frank verdicts and scores for THIS week

### Takeaway
- **Florence is the best-value add-on:** 37 minutes from Bologna, a free and walkable multi-piazza New Year's Eve, world-class art (book around the Mon 28 Dec and 1 Jan closures), and Siena or Pisa as day trips.
- **Rome is the most spectacular and the most halal-friendly**, with Navona to 6 Jan, the St Peter's Square crib, papal events and a likely Circo Massimo concert. But it costs about 4.5–5.5 hours each way from Fano, and the Vatican Museums are closed on two of the eight days. Rome is worth it **only if it is the last stop and they fly home from Rome**, giving 3–4 nights there.
- **A Dolomites detour** gives the best snow and festive alpine atmosphere, but at peak prices with minimum stays and 4–6-hour transfers. It fits only if the traveller drops one city.
- **The Apennines** are a weather-dependent day trip at most.

### Inferences (researcher's judgement)
**Scores for 26 Dec 2026 – 2 Jan 2027 (overall / New Year's Eve)**
