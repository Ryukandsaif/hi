## 1. Weather for each candidate place, 26 Dec – 2 Jan: normals, last year's actuals, fog, bora, acqua alta, and the 2026–27 seasonal outlook

### Takeaway
- **Plan for cold, short, often grey days.** Climatological highs over this week are about:
  - 12–13°C in Rome
  - 10–11°C on the Fano/Pesaro coast
  - 8–9°C in Bologna
  - 6–7°C in Venice and Verona
  - 5–6°C in Milan
  - around or below freezing in Cortina and Val Gardena
- **Night lows:** about 0–2°C in the Po Valley cities (Bologna, Milan, Verona, Venice). Hard frost is normal in the Dolomites.
- **Daylight:** only about 8.6–9.2 hours, with sunset at about 16:30–16:50.
- **What last year showed:** the same week in 2025–26 brought an arctic outbreak around New Year.
  - The Po Valley cities had lows of −3 to −5°C and highs of only 2–4°C on 30 Dec – 3 Jan.
  - Cortina stayed below freezing all day, with highs of −4 to −6°C.
- **This year's outlook:** El Niño is at record strength. The ECMWF/C3S seasonal guidance leans toward a **milder than normal** winter for Italy, wet into December and with less lowland snow than normal. Several Italian commentators flag possible cold outbreaks from January.
- **None of this pins down the specific week.** Day-specific forecasts become useful only about 7–10 days out, from about 16–19 Dec. As late as 24–25 Dec 2025, Italian forecasters were still calling New Year's Eve "uncertain".

**Po Valley fog (Bologna, Milan, Verona, Ferrara)**
- **Milan:** in 1971–2000 the city averaged **93 fog days a year**. In the year before 2018 there were only about 4, under a strict definition — [Il Post, 2018](https://www.ilpost.it/2018/11/05/nebbia-milano/)
- **Long-term decline:** per ISAC-CNR, fog days with visibility under 1 km in Milan fell **73%** in 1991–2000 compared with 1960–69 (per search summary) — [Il Post, Dec 2022](https://www.ilpost.it/2022/12/08/meno-nebbia-di-una-volta-pianura-padana/)
- **Bologna:** winter fog hours in the airport area fell **47%** in 2004–2013 compared with 1984–1994 — [Il Post, Dec 2022](https://www.ilpost.it/2022/12/08/meno-nebbia-di-una-volta-pianura-padana/)

**Venice acqua alta / MOSE**
- **Threshold:** the operational trigger for raising MOSE was **lowered from 120 cm to 110 cm** (Il Post, Sept 2024). Il Post estimated about six times as many raisings as the 32 recorded under the 120 cm rule. This resolves the earlier-notes gap: Wikipedia's 130 cm figure is out of date — [Il Post, 16 Sept 2024](https://www.ilpost.it/2024/09/16/alzare-mose-venezia/)
- **St Mark's Square** is the lowest point and is usually under water from about **80 cm**. This means tides of 80–110 cm can still wet the square even when MOSE is not raised. (This comes from a travel guide, per search summary.) — [Europe for Visitors](https://www.europeforvisitors.com/venice/articles/acqua-alta.htm); [Reid's Italy](https://reidsitaly.com/destinations/veneto/venice/sights/acque_alte.html)
- **Frequency:** tides exceed 110 cm "on average about 4 times a year". This is a low-quality guide figure, and no December-specific statistic was found. Historic December peaks were 166 cm (Dec 1979) and 156 cm (Dec 2008) — [Nomado acqua alta guide](https://www.nomadotravel.app/it/guides/acqua-alta-venice-guide)
- **[OLD] 8 Dec 2020:** MOSE was not raised (works incomplete, forecasts wrong) and Venice flooded — [Il Fatto Quotidiano, 8 Dec 2020](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2020/12/08/il-mose-non-e-attivo-venezia-finisce-sottacqua-lavori-non-ultimati-e-previsioni-sballate-ecco-perche-le-paratoie-non-sono-state-alzate/6030345/)

### Inferences
- **What to tell the traveller honestly [EST]:**
  - **Fano/Pesaro coast:** expect about 9–12°C by day and 2–6°C at night. Rain falls on roughly 1 day in 3, with only about 3 h of sun on average. A NE wind or bora day can make it feel close to freezing and whip up the sea.
  - **Bologna, Verona, Venice, Milan:** expect about 5–9°C by day and around 0°C at night. There may be a few foggy or frosty mornings, with "grey high pressure" (anticyclonic gloom) the classic pattern.
  - **Rome:** the mildest (12–13°C).
  - **Dolomites:** expect −10 to +3°C.
  - **El Niño shift:** the seasonal lean shifts these figures about +1°C. It does not rule out a 2025–26-style arctic outbreak, which put frost in every northern city and below-zero daytime highs in Cortina in this very week.
- **Pack for:** layers to about −5°C, a waterproof shell, waterproof footwear (needed for Venice), and a hat and gloves for New Year's Eve outdoors.
- **Fog as a transport risk (inference):** fog is now far rarer than in the 1970s–90s, but it remains possible in a stagnant high-pressure spell. It mostly affects early-morning flights at Bologna, Verona and Milan, and road driving on the plain (A1, A4, A13, A14 near Bologna). High-speed trains are generally unaffected [BK].
- **Venice tides:** MOSE now rises from forecasts of 110 cm. The realistic Venice risk is therefore a few hours of wet feet in St Mark's Square on an 80–110 cm tide, not a disaster flood. The tide forecast (Centro Maree, Comune di Venezia) is reliable about 2–3 days ahead [BK].
- **Snow in the Dolomites:** a mild El Niño December means likely reliance on artificial snow at lower Dolomite levels, though the high slopes (Sellaronda) are usually skiable by late December [BK]. Val Gardena / Alpe di Siusi is scheduled to open on 4 Dec 2026 (Section 3).
- **Forecast horizon (inference, consistent with the 2025 example):**
  - Reliable day-by-day forecasts start about 7–10 days out, i.e. from about 16–19 Dec for a 26 Dec arrival.
  - New Year's Eve detail firms up only about 3–5 days before, from about 26–28 Dec.
  - Weekly anomaly products (ECMWF extended range) give a useful warmer/colder/wetter signal about 2–3 weeks ahead [BK].
  - Suggest the traveller check ilMeteo, 3BMeteo, MeteoAM or AccuWeather from about 16 Dec, then re-plan Venice and Dolomites days around the forecast.

## 2. Flights from the UAE: nonstops into and out of Italy, open-jaw options, and fares for ~26 Dec out / ~2 Jan back
