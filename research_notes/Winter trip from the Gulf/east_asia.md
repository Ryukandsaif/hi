Source note: Research date 2026-11-03. Most findings come from web-search result summaries (full-page fetches were limited; chinahighlights.com was blocked from this session's network). Where a summary drew on several pages, I cite the page most likely to hold the fact. Many travel-guide sites are secondary or blog-grade; official sources are marked [official] where used. ## Q1. Japan: best 1-week winter combos, snow reliability (Dec vs Jan), New Year closures, and Dec 2026–Jan 2027 events

### Takeaway
Japan offers the most complete winter mix of the three countries: big-city culture and food (Tokyo/Kyoto), very reliable deep snow (Hokkaido/Nagano, best in January), and atmospheric snow villages and onsen. Several marquee events fall outside the window or are now booked-only, though. Sapporo Snow Festival (Feb 4–11, 2027) and Otaru Snow Light Path (Feb 6–13, 2027) are both outside it. The Shirakawa-go light-up runs only on four January evenings and needs a reservation. Ginzan Onsen is moving to timed entry. Avoid Dec 28–Jan 4 unless the traveler specifically wants the New Year atmosphere.

### Cited Findings
**Events and dates**
- Sapporo Snow Festival 2027: **February 4–11, 2027** (8 days) at Odori Park, Tsudome and Susukino. This is **outside a Dec 2026/Jan 2027 window**. — [Sapporo official event list, official](https://www.sapporo.travel/en/event-list/page/2/?cssn%5B0%5D=s-winter); [watabi guide (outline published 13 July 2026)](https://watabi.org/en/articles/sapporo-snow-festival-guide)
- Sources disagree on the edition number (77th vs 78th). Per-site hours and Tsudome details were not final at research time. — [watabi](https://watabi.org/en/articles/sapporo-snow-festival-guide)
- Otaru Snow Light Path is listed for **Feb 6–13, 2027** on the same official page (also outside the window; other sites give slightly different dates). — [sapporo.travel, official](https://www.sapporo.travel/en/event-list/page/2/?cssn%5B0%5D=s-winter)
- Shirakawa-go (UNESCO gassho village) winter light-up 2027: **Jan 11, 17, 24 and 31, 2027, 17:30–19:30**. Entry is **reservation-only** (since 2019), and walk-ups are not admitted. Booking procedures were due in early Sept 2026, so availability by November may be limited. Bus tours from Takayama/Kanazawa/Nagoya that bundle tickets are widely sold. — [Shirakawa-go tourism, official](https://shirakawa-go.gr.jp/en/events/123/); [KKday listing](https://www.kkday.com/en/product/771698); [watabi Shirakawa-go guide](https://watabi.org/en/articles/shirakawa-go-winter-illumination-guide); [japanactivity](https://japanactivity.com/shirakawa-go-winter-light-up)
- Tokyo illuminations: **Marunouchi Illumination Nov 12, 2026–Feb 14, 2027** (Marunouchi Nakadori). The "Marunouchi Bright Holiday" program runs Nov 12–Dec 25, 2026. **Yomiuriland Jewellumination runs Oct 29, 2026–Apr 4, 2027, 16:00–20:30** (paid). Roppongi Hills Keyakizaka dates were not officially confirmed (one guide says it starts Nov 5). — [real-japan-guide Marunouchi](https://real-japan-guide.com/?p=3202); [watabi Tokyo illumination guide](https://watabi.org/en/articles/tokyo-winter-illumination-guide); [todolist-japan](https://todolist-japan.com/en/marunouchi-illumination/)

**Ginzan Onsen (Yamagata), overtourism controls**
- The 2026–27 winter access rules were still a **draft** as of 15 Oct 2026. The plan is park-and-ride, private-car restrictions and **timed entry from Dec 2026 to early Mar 2027**. — [hiddenjapan-gems](https://hiddenjapan-gems.com/ginzan-onsen-where-to-stay/); [selfguidejapan](https://selfguidejapan.com/blog/ginzan-onsen)
- For reference, the 2025–26 rules ran Dec 20–Mar 1. Day-trippers parked at the Taisho Romankan and took a shuttle, with reservations opening 13 days ahead. Sources conflict on the fare (¥500 priority fee vs ¥1,150). The first restrictions (winter 2024–25) capped evening day visitors (reported max 100/hour) and required shuttle reservations. — [Ginzan Onsen association PDF, official](https://www.ginzanonsen.jp/%E3%80%90%E8%8B%B1%E4%B8%AD%E8%AA%9E%E3%80%912025%E5%86%AC%E3%83%9E%E3%82%A4%E3%82%AB%E3%83%BC%E8%A6%8F%E5%88%B6%E6%A1%88%E5%86%852.pdf); [Time Out Tokyo, Nov 2024](https://www.timeout.com/tokyo/news/ginzan-onsen-to-limit-daytime-visitors-this-winter-to-preserve-its-charm-111924); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/east-asia/article/3291953/japans-picturesque-ginzan-onsen-starts-limiting-day-trippers-tackle-overtourism)

**Snow reliability, Dec vs Jan**
- Niseko United (Hokkaido): all four areas are scheduled to open **28 Nov 2026**, subject to snow. The peak-price period is **Dec 24–Feb 28**. — [niseko.ne.jp, official](https://www.niseko.ne.jp/en/all-mountain-season-pass); [japanactivity lift-pass guide](https://japanactivity.com/niseko-lift-pass-guide)
- Hakuba (Nagano): JMA station data (1991–2020, station at about 700 m) give an average of **216 cm of snowfall in December vs 174 cm in January**. December is the heaviest month at the valley station, though summit snow is much deeper. — [japanuncharted](https://japanuncharted.com/nagano/ski/hakuba-snowfall-season)
- Early December can be thin. The 2025–26 Niseko start was slow, with limited powder terrain in early December, and SkiAsia reported a past Niseko "worst start to winter in 67 years" followed by a very snowy January. Good early years do happen (about 142 cm by Dec 17 in one year). — [SkiAsia](https://skiasia.com/news/has-niseko-just-recorded-its-worst-start-to-winter-in-67-years/); [Japan Ski Experience season guide](https://japanskiexperience.com/blog/japan-ski-season-guide/)
- January is the usual powder peak. One analysis found that **77% of January ski weeks in Niseko had at least 2 days with 6+ inches of new snow**. Niseko gets roughly 15–20 m a season vs Hakuba's roughly 11 m (approximate; sources disagree). — [Snow Season Central](https://www.snowseasoncentral.com/?p=3127); [japanuncharted](https://japanuncharted.com/nagano/ski/hakuba-snowfall-season)

**Japanese New Year (approx. Dec 28/29–Jan 3/4)**
- The country runs on a reduced schedule from roughly **Dec 28/29 to Jan 4**, with the deepest closures **Jan 1–3**. Many shops and restaurants, museums and banks close. Department stores typically close Jan 1, though some big-city stores now open. Examples: Hamarikyu Gardens closes Dec 29–Jan 1 and Osaka Castle Dec 28–Jan 1. — [hiddenjapan-gems 2026–27 closures](https://hiddenjapan-gems.com/japan-new-year-closures-2026-2027/); [Truly Tokyo](https://trulytokyo.com/new-years-tokyo/); [Kyoto official closures notice](https://kyoto.travel/es/news/new-year-closures)
- Konbini, chain fast food, shrines and temples, hotels/ryokan, public transport and taxis stay open. Hotel restaurants are the reliable dining option. Shrines and temples are packed for **hatsumōde** (first prayer of the year, millions of visitors). — [fun-japan](https://fun-japan.jp/en/articles/7669); [Flight Centre Tokyo hours](https://www.flightcentre.co.uk/travel-guides/japan/tokyo/hours-of-operation)
- Trains and roads are "incredibly congested" just before and after New Year's Day. One Tokyo guide says holiday-peak prices run 2–3x (a general figure covering Golden Week/Obon/New Year, so treat it as indicative). — [fun-japan](https://fun-japan.jp/en/articles/7669); [whattime-city Tokyo holidays](https://whattime-city.pages.dev/tokyo/guide/holidays)

### Inferences
- **Best combos for this traveler (my assessment):**
  1. **Tokyo (3–4 nights) + Hokkaido (3 nights: Sapporo/Otaru, or Niseko if they ski)**, flying Tokyo–Sapporo rather than taking the train. This has the strongest "snow + city" contrast. Best mid-to-late January, when Niseko powder peaks and Christmas/New Year pricing has eased. Snow festivals are not in the window, but Sapporo/Otaru are snowy and atmospheric throughout January.
  2. **Tokyo + Nagano (Hakuba skiing and/or Jigokudani snow monkeys + Nozawa/Shibu onsen)**. Shinkansen access from Tokyo makes this the most time-efficient snow combo (travel times not verified in this session). Fits a 6–7 day trip with no internal flight.
  3. **Kyoto (2–3 nights) + Takayama/Shirakawa-go (1–2 nights) + Tokyo**. The most "culture" option, and worth anchoring on one of the four light-up dates (Jan 11/17/24/31) if a reservation or bus-tour seat can still be had. Kyoto's new hotel tax adds noticeably at luxury rates (see Q2).
  4. Ginzan Onsen (Yamagata) works as a 1-night add-on from Tokyo, but **staying overnight at a ryokan is the only reliable way to see it at night** given timed entry for day-trippers. Ryokan there are tiny and book out early.
- **Best timing within the window:** **Jan 5–31, 2027** gives the best balance: reliable snow, post-holiday prices, all businesses open, Shirakawa-go light-up dates, and Tokyo illuminations still running. Early-to-mid December is cheaper, with full city festive lights (Christmas illuminations), but carries a thin-snow risk in ski areas. Dec 24–Jan 4 is the most expensive and crowded period and has closures, but it does have a unique New Year atmosphere (Joya-no-kane bells, hatsumōde).

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session: Jigokudani snow monkey park conditions, Sapporo White Illumination 2026 dates (usually late Nov–Dec 25 on Odori; unverified), Hakodate/Otaru December events, Kyoto New Year crowding specifics, and exact Tokyo–Nagano/Tokyo–Sapporo transit times.
- I found no official notice on whether 2027 Shirakawa-go light-up reservations are sold out.
- 2026–27 Ginzan Onsen final rules, fares and booking links were unpublished at research time.

## Q2. Japan: 2026 changes affecting tourists (departure tax, Kyoto accommodation tax, JESTA, overtourism, JR Pass, yen level)

### Takeaway
Japan got noticeably more expensive at the margins in 2026: the departure tax tripled to ¥3,000, Kyoto's tiered hotel tax now reaches ¥10,000 per person per night at luxury rates, and the JR Pass rose about 6%. Hotel rates are at record highs. JESTA does **not** apply in this window (target FY2028). The yen remains historically weak (about 156/USD in Sept 2026), which largely offsets these increases for a USD/AED-pegged Gulf traveler.

### Cited Findings
- **International Tourist Tax (departure tax): ¥1,000 → ¥3,000 per departure from July 1, 2026.** Tickets issued on or before June 30, 2026 keep the old rate (but changes to a post-July-1 departure trigger the new rate). Under-2s are exempt, and the tax is usually built into the air fare. — [Japan Tourism Agency notice PDF, official](https://www.mlit.go.jp/kankocho/content/001996466.pdf); [JNTO / japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/ca/news/japans-international-tourist-tax-is-increasing/); [Peach Aviation](https://www.flypeach.com/en/news/20260525); [Travel Voice, FY2026 tax outline](https://www.travelvoice.jp/english/the-japanese-ruling-parties-specify-a-rise-in-departure-tax-to-3-000-yen-in-the-tax-reform-outline-for-fy2026)
- **Kyoto City accommodation tax (from March 1, 2026), per person per night, based on the room rate excluding meals and consumption tax:** under ¥6,000 → ¥200; ¥6,000–<¥20,000 → ¥400; ¥20,000–<¥50,000 → ¥1,000 (was ¥500); ¥50,000–<¥100,000 → ¥4,000; ¥100,000+ → **¥10,000** (Japan's highest). — [Kyoto official tax-change page (KO)](https://kyoto.travel/ko/news/tax-change); [Kyoto official (EN)](https://kyoto.travel/en/news/tax-change/); [Time Out, Mar 2026](https://timeout.com/osaka/news/kyotos-highest-hotel-tax-hike-takes-effect-all-you-need-to-know-030226); [machiya-inn-japan](https://www.machiya-inn-japan.com/kyoto-tax/). Caveat: the upper two tiers were confirmed on the Korean-language official page and in press, but the English official page snippet did not show the full table.
- **JESTA (Japan's planned ESTA-style pre-travel authorization):** the target is "by the end of FY2028" (April 2028–March 2029), brought forward from 2030. The fee, process and eligible list (71 vs 74 visa-exempt countries reported) are not published. **It is not in effect for Dec 2026/Jan 2027**, and existing visa/visa-exemption rules apply. — [VisasNews](https://visasnews.com/en/jesta-what-we-know-about-japans-future-electronic-travel-authorization/); [GoAsia, Aug 2026](https://goasia.cc/en/news/japan-immigration-plan-jesta-2028-travel-authorization); [YMMV, Mar 2026](https://yourmileagemayvary.com/2026/03/26/japan-is-changing-entry-rules-for-americans-but-not-right-away)
- A headline also reports Japan's visa fees rising sharply from July 1, 2026. This matters only for nationalities needing a visa and is out of scope here (visas are covered by another researcher). — [VisasNews Japan news](https://visasnews.com/en/asia-news/japan-news/)
- **Japan Rail Pass (nationwide), from Oct 1, 2026:** 7-day Ordinary **¥50,000 → ¥53,000** (about USD 340); 7-day Green **¥70,000 → ¥74,000** (about USD 475). These are third-party figures and the official site was not checked. — [Tokyo International Meetup](https://tokyointernationalmeetup.com/japan-rail-pass-price-increase-october-2026-guide/); [Real Japan Guide](https://real-japan-guide.com/?p=48); [JNTO Canada news](https://www.japan.travel/en/ca/news/price-increases-are-coming-to-the-nationwide-japan-rail-pass-2026/)
- **Yen level:** FRED monthly averages are **156.42 JPY/USD in Sept 2026**, 158.85 in Aug and 162.33 in July 2026. No confirmed October figure was found, and forecast sites put Oct–Nov around 155–161 (projections only). — [FRED EXJPUS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/EXJPUS)
- **Hotel price inflation:** STR reported Japan's April 2026 ADR at **¥23,397 (about USD 160)**, a record and +4.6% over April 2025. MetroEngines reported a national listed ADR record of **¥32,340 in May 2026**. Listed OTA ADRs rose about **14–17% YoY in Tokyo** and **18–20% in Kyoto** in Apr–May 2026, with growth attributed to US/European demand as Chinese arrivals fell about 15%. JLL notes growth decelerating in Q1 2026. — [HotelBank inbound coverage](https://en.hotelbank.jp/category/inbound/); [Nikkei-syndicated report (lavx)](https://news.lavx.hu/article/japan-s-hotel-rates-surge-on-western-tourists-as-chinese-arrivals-slump); [JLL APPD](https://research.jllapsites.com/?p=36037)
- Overtourism measures relevant to this window: Ginzan Onsen timed entry and park-and-ride (Q1), the reservation-only Shirakawa-go light-up (Q1), and the Kyoto tax (above). — sources as cited above

### Inferences
- For a typical 1-week, two-base trip (e.g., Tokyo + Sapporo), **the JR Pass is unlikely to pay off**. A domestic flight Tokyo–Sapporo plus local transit (Suica) is faster and probably cheaper. It pays off only on rail-heavy itineraries (e.g., Tokyo → Kanazawa → Kyoto → Tokyo, or Tokyo → Nagano → Kyoto).
- At a 5-star Kyoto room costing ¥100,000+/night, two adults pay **¥20,000/night (about USD 128) in tax alone**. At ¥50,000–99,999 rooms it is ¥8,000/night for two (about USD 51). This is material but not deal-breaking.
- Because AED, QAR, SAR and the other GCC currencies are effectively USD-pegged, the weak yen makes Japan unusually good value in hard-currency terms, even with 2026's tax and price increases.

### Gaps
- I did not find official JR Pass site confirmation of the post-Oct-1 price, or whether the "buy before the deadline at the old price" window has closed.
- No Tokyo-only STR ADR for December was found. Dec/Jan seasonal ADR for Tokyo/Sapporo/Niseko is not verified.
- Other 2026 measures were not researched (e.g., Kyoto Gion private-lane restrictions, Mt Fuji fees, which are off-season anyway).

## Q3. South Korea: Seoul + ski resorts, Jan 2027 winter festivals, food scene, costs. Is it compelling vs Japan?

### Takeaway
Korea is a credible, cheaper and shorter-haul alternative for a **Seoul-centric** trip with 1–2 nights of snow (Pyeongchang/Gangwon skiing, ice-fishing festivals). Its snow quality, scenery and range of "winter atmosphere" experiences are clearly a tier below Japan's, and its top winter festivals (Hwacheon, Pyeongchang Trout) start around **Jan 9, 2027**, so a **December trip misses them**. Halal-certified dining is thin outside Itaewon, though Muslim-friendly infrastructure is improving.

### Cited Findings
**Festivals (Jan 2027; dates tentative, not officially confirmed at research time)**
- **Hwacheon Sancheoneo Ice Festival 2027:** most listings give **Jan 9–31, 2027** (tentative). Mileasia gives Jan 9–Feb 1 (approximate). Day tours from Seoul are sold for Jan 9–31. Hwacheon County launched a global marketing campaign for the 2027 edition in May 2026. — [Seoulstart](https://seoulstart.com/festivals/hwacheon-sancheoneo-ice-festival); [Mileasia](https://mileasia.com/korea/winter-festivals/); [Trazy tour](https://www.trazy.com/ms-MY/experience/detail/hwacheon-sancheoneo-ice-festival-eobi-ice-valley-tour); [Asia Economy, May 2026](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/social-general/2026052010064844070)
- **Pyeongchang Trout Festival 2027:** tentatively **Jan 9–Feb 9, 2027** (Seoulstart). Mileasia gives Jan 9–Feb 1, and a weekday tour runs Jan 11–Feb 11. The 2026 edition was **delayed from Jan 1 to Jan 9 due to abnormal (warm) weather**, which shows the dates depend on the weather. Official site: festival700.or.kr. — [Seoulstart](https://seoulstart.com/festivals/pyeongchang-trout-festival); [Asia Economy, Dec 2025](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2025122214140519558); [iVisitKorea](https://www.ivisitkorea.com/pyeongchang-trout-festival/)

**Skiing and costs**
- Yongpyong (Pyeongchang) 2025–26 season ran **Nov 22, 2025–Mar 18, 2026**. Low season was Nov 22–Dec 23 and Feb 22–Mar 8, implying that **Dec 24–Feb 21 is peak**. — [Trazy Yongpyong package](https://www.trazy.com/experience/detail/yongpyong-ski-resort-overnight-package)
- Lift prices (mostly undated listings, so treat as estimates): Yongpyong peak adult day ticket **₩123,000** (undated); Yongpyong afternoon–night pass (12:30–22:00) ₩89,000; High1 day pass ₩66,000–70,000 (undated Trazy listing); a general guide gives about ₩80,000–100,000 for lift passes and ₩30,000–40,000/day for rentals. Locals get 30–40% discounts via Korean cards or rental shops. — [snow-online](https://www.snow-online.com/ski-resort/yongpyong_lift-ticket-prices.html); [Time Out Seoul](https://www.timeout.com/seoul/sports-and-fitness/comparing-ski-resorts); [Trazy High1](https://www.trazy.com/experience/detail/high1-ski-resort-korea-ski-tour-package); [Korea Experience](https://koreaexperience.com/blog/winter-sports-skiing-snowboarding-and-ice-climbing)
- High1 began selling a 2026–27 **"X6+" multi-resort season pass on Sept 9, 2026**, covering six resorts including High1 and Yongpyong (price not found). — [Financial News](https://en.fnnews.com/news/202609091736136054)

**Muslim-friendly (more in Q6)**
- Seoul's Myeongdong street market has been actively courting Muslim tourists (2025). — [The National, Apr 2025](https://thenationalnews.com/news/asia/2025/04/18/salam-alaykum-how-seouls-most-popular-street-market-is-welcoming-muslim-tourists)

### Inferences
- **Compelling vs Japan? Generally no, for this traveler, unless:** (a) the main draw is Seoul itself (K-culture, shopping, palaces, cafés, modern food), (b) they have been to Japan already, or (c) they want lower costs and a slightly shorter flight. On ski quality, snow scenery, onsen culture and the breadth of winter experiences, Japan is clearly stronger. Korean resorts are known for being cold but relying heavily on machine-made snow. This is background knowledge and was not verified here.
- **Timing:** in Korea, **mid-to-late January (Jan 9–31)** is the only part of the window that combines the ice festivals with skiing. A December Korea trip is "Seoul in the cold + Christmas lights + a ski day", which is pleasant but not distinctive.
- Seoul Lunar New Year (Seollal) 2027 coincides with Feb 6, 2027, outside the window (inferred from the shared lunar calendar; see Q4).
- Typical shape: 4–5 nights Seoul + 2 nights Pyeongchang (Yongpyong/Alpensia) with a Hwacheon or Pyeongchang festival day. Lift costs (about USD 55–90/day) are below Niseko peak (about USD 86/day) but in the same broad range.

### Gaps
- No Seoul 4–5 star hotel rates for Dec 2026/Jan 2027 were found (e.g., Four Seasons, Signiel, Lotte, Josun Palace). Needs booking-site checks.
- Official 2026–27 lift prices for Yongpyong/High1 were not found, and the Korean festivals' official 2027 dates were not yet published.
- I found no data on Korea's snow reliability, Seoul December events (e.g., Seoul Lantern/winter festivals), or K-food cost benchmarks.

## Q4. China: Harbin Ice and Snow World 2026–27 dates, Harbin Festival, Yabuli, Beijing in winter, practicalities, Chinese New Year 2027

### Takeaway
Harbin is the most spectacular **pure ice-and-snow festival** experience in the window. Ice and Snow World should open around **mid-to-late Dec 2026** (unconfirmed), and the city festival opens **Jan 5, 2027**. It is brutally cold (January average high about −13 °C, low about −24 °C). Paired with Beijing (Forbidden City, Great Wall), it is a distinctive 1-week "Beijing + Harbin" trip, best **early-to-mid January 2027**. It suits adventurous travelers comfortable with China's app-based payment ecosystem and internet restrictions. Chinese New Year (Feb 6, 2027) is outside the window, but the Spring Festival travel rush likely starts in late January.

### Cited Findings
- **Ice and Snow World 2026–27:** the operator confirmed the **28th edition** on 12 Oct 2026 "for this winter" but, as of 19 Oct 2026, had published **no opening date or ticket price**. Guides **estimate opening around Dec 20–24, 2026** (not official). Last year the date was announced only about 2 days before opening. — [TripChina.me](https://tripchina.me/harbin-ice-festival-guide/); [Homeground China](https://homegroundchina.com/guides/harbin-ice-festival/); [Trip.com guide](https://www.trip.com/guide/activity/harbin-ice-festival-2027.html)
- **Last season for comparison:** opened **Dec 17, 2025** (10:00) and closed **Feb 21, 2026** (cut short by an unusually warm spell). — [you.co guide](https://www.you.co/sg/blog/harbin-ice-festival/); [Yellowbird Tour](https://www.yellowbirdtour.com/?p=6083)
- **Ticket price (2025–26):** standard adult **¥328 CNY (about USD 46)**, unchanged despite the park expanding to **1.2 million m²**. A promotional **¥298** was sold in advance Dec 17–23, 2025. — [Belt and Road portal (Xinhua)](https://eng.yidaiyilu.gov.cn/p/0IQ17IGR.html); [China Daily, Nov 2025](https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202511/27/WS6937fb5e498e368550338a73/harbins-winter-wonderland-takes-shape-with-record-use-of-ice-and-snow.html); [Australasian Leisure Management](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/worlds-largest-ice-and-snow-theme-park-set-to-open-in-northeast-china)
- **Harbin International Ice and Snow Festival:** the ceremony was held **Jan 5, 2026**, and **Jan 5, 2027** is expected for the next one. It is a city-wide event separate from the park's opening date. — [TripChina.me](https://tripchina.me/harbin-ice-festival-guide/); [Trip.com](https://www.trip.com/guide/activity/harbin-ice-festival-2027.html)
- **Cold:** January average daily high **about −13 °C**, average low **about −24 °C** (another source gives a January mean of −24.8 °C, likely a different metric). — [China Highlights Harbin January weather](https://www.chinahighlights.com/harbin/weather/january.htm); [ChinaTravel Harbin weather](https://www.chinatravel.com/harbin/weather)
- **Yabuli (China's largest ski resort, Heilongjiang):** the resort's operating-season average temperature is given as about −10 °C (another source says about −16 °C). Prices vary by source and date: **¥140/2h, ¥280/4h, ¥380/full day** (higher at weekends/holidays), or a range of ¥120–480. These are travel-guide figures that may not reflect 2026. — [ChinaTravel Yabuli](https://www.chinatravel.com/harbin/attraction/yabuli-ski-resort); [China Xian Tour Yabuli](https://www.chinaxiantour.com/yabuli-international-ski-resort.html)
- **Beijing–Harbin high-speed rail:** the fastest G-trains take about **4h33m (G101, Feb 2026 timetable)** to about 5h20m, depending on the source, from **Beijing Chaoyang station**. — [Trip.com Beijing–Harbin train](https://sg.trip.com/guide/train/beijing-to-harbin-train.html); [ChinaTravel train page](https://www.chinatravel.com/china-trains/beijing-harbin-train); [China Highlights train page](https://www.chinahighlights.com/china-trains/beijing-harbin-train.htm)
- **Payments and tickets:** 12306 (the national rail booking system) takes Chinese cards, Alipay or WeChat Pay. Foreigners can **bind an overseas Visa/Mastercard to Alipay/WeChat Pay** and pay that way. Trip.com offers English booking and accepts foreign cards (at slightly higher prices). Passport verification on 12306 takes minutes to days (sources conflict). Caveat: mostly blog-grade sources. — [China travel guide (blog)](https://china-travel-site.pages.dev/blog/02-how-to-buy-china-train-tickets/); [Trip.com](https://sg.trip.com/guide/train/beijing-to-harbin-train.html)
- **Chinese New Year 2027: Saturday, February 6, 2027** (Year of the Goat). The statutory holiday is most often given as **Feb 5–11, 2027**. It falls **outside the Dec/Jan window**. — [ExamineChina CNY 2027](https://www.examinechina.com/chinese-new-year-2027/); [ExamineChina holidays 2027](https://www.examinechina.com/holidays-in-china-2027/)

### Inferences

## Q6. Muslim-friendly considerations: halal food, prayer facilities, popularity with Gulf travelers

### Takeaway
Japan and Korea rank similarly mid-table as non-OIC Muslim-friendly destinations (GMTI 2026: Japan #36, Korea #39 globally). Both have halal and prayer-room resources concentrated in the capitals (Tokyo; Seoul/Itaewon) and much thinner coverage in ski and rural areas. Halal *certified* food remains limited in both, and travelers should plan meals (seafood/vegetarian fallbacks, halal-listed restaurants, hotel concierge help). China's Hui Muslim cuisine makes halal food arguably easier to find in Beijing/Harbin, but this was not verified in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **GMTI 2026 (Mastercard–CrescentRating):** **Japan #36 globally (score 53), up 8 places from #44 in 2025**, strongest pillar Environment 73/100. **South Korea #39 globally (score 52), #10 among non-OIC destinations**, Environment 75, Communication 35.3. In GMTI 2025, Japan was 14th among non-OIC destinations. — [CrescentRating Japan](https://crescentrating.com/insights/gmti/destinations/japan); [CrescentRating South Korea](https://crescentrating.com/insights/gmti/destinations/south-korea); [Honichi, Jul 2025](https://honichi.com/news/2025/07/23/muslim_report2025_mastercard/)
- **Japan:** JNTO's Muslim-traveler guide points to Halal Media Japan (halal restaurants, mosques, prayer spaces), Halal Gourmet Japan and HalalJapan.jp. Prayer rooms exist at major airports, in some department stores (e.g., Takashimaya Shinjuku) and at a small number of Muslim-friendly hotels. Halal restaurants and producers remain few, and some "Muslim-friendly" places cook in shared kitchens. **Japan has no central halal accreditation body**, so certification quality varies. — [JNTO Muslim travelers](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/muslim-travelers/); [japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e2302.html); [Japan Travel, Takashimaya prayer room](https://en.japantravel.com/article/prayer-room-at-takashimaya-shinjuku/20901); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/being-halal-in-japan?amp=1)
- **Korea:** KTO uses a 4-tier system (halal certified / self-certified / Muslim-friendly / pork-free), dating from 2016. Only about **15 restaurants nationwide held KMF Halal Committee certification (late 2025)**. Itaewon is the hub, home to **Seoul Central Mosque**. KTO's directory reportedly maps **180+ prayer rooms and mosques** (older counts were about 40). Incheon Airport has 24-hour prayer rooms in T1 and T2. A 2024 report found many halal maps outdated. — [Seoulstart halal guide](https://seoulstart.com/guides/halal-food-korea-guide); [Unpacking Korea, Oct 2025](https://unpackingkorea.blogspot.com/2025/10/halal-food-in-seoul-prayer-rooms-halal.html); [Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/697176/international/korea/halal-travel-in-korea-a-guide-to-muslim-friendly-destinations); [Aju Daily, 2024](https://www.ajudaily.com/view/20240516141032444)

### Inferences
- For a Gulf family, **Tokyo and Seoul are workable for halal dining with planning**. Hokkaido ski towns, Nagano/Hakuba, Shirakawa-go, Ginzan and Pyeongchang resorts will have very few certified-halal options. Ryokan kaiseki meals (often with dashi, mirin and sake) need advance requests. Expect to rely on seafood, vegetarian options and self-catering.
- Alcohol-centric après-ski and izakaya culture is prominent in Niseko and Hakuba. Family-oriented resorts and city hotels may suit conservative travelers better.
- China: Hui Muslim (Chinese Muslim) restaurants are common across northern Chinese cities, so Beijing/Harbin may in practice be the *easiest* of the three for everyday halal meals (an inference from background knowledge, not verified here).

## Q7. Is one week too short, given about 9–11 hour flights and jet lag? Realistic on-the-ground time

### Takeaway
A week is workable but tight. Realistically it gives **5–6 usable days on the ground** for Japan/Korea (and about 6 for China, with its shorter flight and smaller time difference). That supports **two bases at most** (one city + one snow destination). Three bases (e.g., Tokyo + Kyoto + Hokkaido) is only realistic at 9 days.

### Cited Findings
- No sources were gathered on flight durations (covered by the separate flights researcher). The facts relevant to ground time are internal transfer times: the Beijing–Harbin G-train takes about 4.5–5.3h. — [Trip.com](https://sg.trip.com/guide/train/beijing-to-harbin-train.html)
- January in Harbin averages −13 °C high and −24 °C low, which limits how much outdoor sightseeing fits into a day. — [China Highlights](https://www.chinahighlights.com/harbin/weather/january.htm)

### Inferences
- **Time zones (background):** Japan and Korea are UTC+9, which is 5h ahead of the UAE/Oman (UTC+4) and 6h ahead of Saudi Arabia/Qatar/Kuwait/Bahrain (UTC+3). China is UTC+8 (4–5h ahead). Eastbound overnight flights typically land in the morning, so day 1 is a low-energy half day. Return flights from North Asia to the Gulf are often late-night departures, which saves the last day.
- **Realistic schedule for a 7-night trip:** Day 1 arrival (half day), Days 2–6 full, Day 7 full or half plus a night flight. That is about 5.5–6 effective days. Each internal transfer (Tokyo↔Sapporo flight, Tokyo↔Nagano Shinkansen, Beijing↔Harbin train) costs about half a day.
- **Recommendation:** for 5–6 nights, choose one city plus a snow day trip (e.g., Tokyo + a Nagano snow-monkey day trip, Seoul + a Hwacheon/Pyeongchang day trip, or Beijing + Harbin by train). For 7–9 nights, do two bases. Avoid trying to combine skiing, Kyoto and Tokyo in under 9 days.
- The 5–6h eastward time shift is moderate. Most travelers adjust in 1–2 days, and the return westward is easier.

## Q8. Frank per-country assessment: strengths, weaknesses, who it suits

### Takeaway
**Japan is the strongest all-round choice** for a traveler wanting culture, cities, food, nature/adventure and snow in one week, ideally **mid-to-late January 2027**. **China (Beijing + Harbin)** is the most spectacular and distinctive pure "ice festival" trip, best **Jan 1–20, 2027**, but harder logistically and extremely cold. **Korea** is the value/Seoul-lover's option, compelling mainly for K-culture fans and only distinctive for winter from **Jan 9, 2027** onward.

### Cited Findings
- Japan: deep, reliable January powder (77% of Niseko January weeks with 2+ powder days); illuminations through Feb 14 (Marunouchi) and Apr 4 (Yomiuriland); Shirakawa-go light-up Jan 11/17/24/31; Sapporo Snow Festival Feb 4–11 (outside the window); New Year closures Dec 28–Jan 4; 2026 cost increases (departure tax ¥3,000, Kyoto tax up to ¥10,000/person/night, JR Pass ¥53,000); weak yen (about 156/USD). — sources in Q1, Q2
- Korea: festivals tentatively Jan 9–31 (Hwacheon) and Jan 9–Feb 9 (Pyeongchang), both weather-dependent; lift tickets about ₩66k–123k; only about 15 KMF-certified halal restaurants nationwide. — sources in Q3, Q6
- China: Ice and Snow World expected around Dec 20–24 (unconfirmed); festival opening Jan 5, 2027; ticket ¥328; −13/−24 °C January; Beijing–Harbin about 4.5–5.3h by rail; CNY Feb 6, 2027 (outside the window). — sources in Q4

### Inferences (my assessment)
**Japan**
- *Strengths:* the best combination of all three of the traveler's interests. World-class food, very safe and easy, and excellent winter variety (powder skiing, onsen, snow villages, illuminations, Tokyo/Kyoto culture). The weak yen makes it good value for USD-pegged currencies. Japan's GMTI ranking is improving.
- *Weaknesses:* Sapporo Snow Festival falls just outside the window. Peak-period (Dec 24–Jan 3) prices and crowds are high, with New Year closures. Niseko luxury is very expensive at peak. Hotel prices are at records, and the 2026 taxes add up. Halal-certified dining is limited outside Tokyo. The best atmospheric spots (Shirakawa-go, Ginzan) now require reservations or timed entry.
- *Suits:* first-timers to East Asia, families, couples, food lovers, skiers, and anyone wanting "snow + culture" in one trip. **Verdict: worth it, and the top pick.** Mid-to-late January (or early-to-mid December for city festive lights with a smaller snow element).

**South Korea**
- *Strengths:* Seoul's energy, shopping, K-culture, palaces and food. Somewhat cheaper than Japan. Fun ice-fishing festivals in January. Ski resorts within 2–3h of Seoul. A reasonable Muslim-friendly infrastructure in Seoul (Itaewon, KTO directory, Incheon prayer rooms).
- *Weaknesses:* snow and ski terrain are less impressive than Japan's. Festivals don't start until about Jan 9 and are weather-dependent (the 2026 Pyeongchang edition was delayed). December has less winter distinctiveness. Halal-certified dining is very thin outside Itaewon.
- *Suits:* K-pop/K-drama fans, repeat Asia travelers, budget-conscious families, and those prioritising city life with a taste of snow. **Verdict: worth it mainly for Seoul lovers, and second-tier as a "winter" trip.** Go Jan 9–31 if the festivals matter.

**China (Beijing + Harbin)**
- *Strengths:* the world's largest ice-and-snow festival, unique and highly photogenic, combined with Beijing's imperial heritage (Forbidden City, Great Wall). Inexpensive luxury hotels and entry (park ticket about USD 46). Halal Hui cuisine is likely available (unverified). The shortest flights of the three, and the smallest time shift.
- *Weaknesses:* extreme cold (−20 °C to −30 °C at night) is a real hardship for Gulf travelers. The park's opening date is uncertain until days before, so December is risky. Payment and internet hurdles (Alipay/WeChat Pay setup, app blocking; internet not verified here). Language barriers. Busy around Jan 5 and from late January (the chunyun rush ahead of the Feb 6 New Year). Food scene and nature/adventure are narrower than Japan's (Yabuli is the only big ski add-on).
- *Suits:* adventurous travelers, photographers, repeat visitors to Asia, and those wanting a once-in-a-lifetime ice spectacle with a heritage city. **Verdict: worth it for the "wow" factor**, best Jan 1–20, 2027. Less ideal for young children or anyone cold-averse.

### Gaps
- These assessments rest partly on background knowledge (Korean snow quality, China internet and payment friction, halal availability in China) that was not fully source-verified in this session. See the per-question gaps above.
